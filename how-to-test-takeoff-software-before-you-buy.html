How to test drywall takeoff software before you buy

By The ReadMyBlueprint Team · Nov 28, 2026

Test it on a plan set you have already bid, where you know the right answer. Run the same floor through every tool, compare stud, track and sheet counts against your own numbers, plant one unclear wall to see whether the tool flags it or guesses, and score each one on the same rubric before you open the next.

A vendor's sample drawings are clean on purpose. Your plans have the scanned addendum, the legend split across two sheets and the partition with no tag. That is the job the software has to do, so that is what you test.

Which plan set should you use?

Pick a recent job with three things in it:

  • Numbers you trust. Your own stud, track and sheet counts for at least one floor, plus the total you submitted.
  • One scanned page. A raster sheet is where many tools stop reading text. If every sheet in your test is a clean vector PDF, you have not tested the hard part.
  • A repeated unit. Multi-family or hotel work with a typical unit shows whether the tool counts a repeated layout once per unit or makes you redo it.

The ten checks

Run every tool through the same list, on the same floor.

  1. Legend reading. Does it read your wall type legend, or do you rebuild every assembly by hand? Ask what happens when a legend row is not in its library.
  2. Counts, not just linear feet. Get studs, track and sheets for one corridor. If it stops at LF, every count after that is still your formula.
  3. Corners and intersections. Check whether extra studs at corners and T-intersections appear anywhere. The on-center formula alone misses them (see corners and intersections).
  4. Openings. Are door and window openings deducted from board, and do jambs add studs?
  5. Repeated units. Time one typical unit, then check the multiplied total.
  6. Planted ambiguity. Pick a wall with no type tag or a tag that disagrees with the finish schedule. Does the tool flag it, or quietly assign a type?
  7. Traceability. Pick three numbers at random. Can you find the sheet and the wall each came from in under five minutes?
  8. Insulation and accessories. If you bid them, do batts, sound attenuation and corner bead come through as their own lines?
  9. Export. Open the export before you pay. You want one row per wall or wall type, not a single total.
  10. Limits. Page count, file size and sheet scale handling. A 300-sheet set is the job you bought it for.

How do you score it?

Rate each line 1 to 10 and multiply by the weight. The weights below lean on the two things your bid actually stands on: whether you can check a number, and whether you get counts you can price.

CriterionWeightA 10 looks like
Traceability3.0Every count links to a sheet and a wall
Trade outputs2.5Studs, track and sheets per wall type
Unclear walls2.0Flagged for you, never silently guessed
Export and workflow1.5Into your bid sheet in one step
Trial access1.0Your own PDF, no sales call first

Worked example. A tool traces walls well (8 × 3.0 = 24) but gives linear feet only (4 × 2.5 = 10), guesses at the planted wall (3 × 2.0 = 6), exports a messy CSV (5 × 1.5 = 7.5) and needs a booked demo (2 × 1.0 = 2). Total: 49.5 out of 100. Our own cut-off: 70 or more is worth a second test on a full set, under 60 is a no.

How long does the test take?

Plan on about 40 minutes per tool: 10 to load the set and find the legend, 15 for one floor, 10 to compare counts with yours, 5 to score. Do the same floor in each tool back to back while the wall types are fresh, and score each before you open the next one.

When is a spreadsheet still the better choice?

If you bid only a few sets a month and your wall types rarely change, a spreadsheet you built and trust may beat any tool. The cost shows up as formula drift: when one cell changes, nothing tells you which old bid used the old number. A formula reference helps keep it honest.

FAQ

Should I test on the vendor's sample plans?

No. Sample sets are chosen to work. Use a job you already bid so you can compare against your own numbers.

How close should the counts be?

Set your own threshold before you start, per wall type, not for the job total. A job total can look right while two wall types are wrong in opposite directions.

What is the single most useful check?

The planted unclear wall. A tool that guesses quietly puts an error in your bid that you never see.
