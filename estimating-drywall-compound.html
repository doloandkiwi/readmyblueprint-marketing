Estimating drywall compound: from board area to pails of mud

By The ReadMyBlueprint Team · Nov 24, 2026 · Updated Nov 24, 2026

USG publishes a coverage of about 10 gallons of ready-mixed all-purpose compound per 1,000 sq ft of board, and about 9.4 gallons for its lightweight Plus 3. So gallons are board square feet divided by 100, a 4x8 sheet takes roughly a third of a gallon, and one 4.5-gallon pail finishes about 450 sq ft of board.

Those figures are for one manufacturer's product, with no waste added. This page shows how to turn your board area into pails, what the finish level does to it, and where the numbers stop being published.

How much joint compound does 1,000 sq ft of board take?

Two USG submittal sheets give the coverage directly: its Sheetrock all-purpose compound sheet lists approximately 10 gal./1,000 sq. ft. of gypsum panels, and the Plus 3 lightweight sheet lists approximately 9.4. USG's own FAQ says about two 4.5-gallon pails finish 1,000 sq ft with its UltraLightweight compound, and that the rate is comparable to its other ready-mix compounds.

  • Per square foot: 10 / 1,000 = 0.01 gal.
  • Per 4x8 sheet (32 sq ft): 0.32 gal.
  • Per 4.5-gal pail: 4.5 / 0.01 = 450 sq ft of board.

The sheets we read do not say which finish level the figure assumes. Treat it as a planning number, then check it against your own jobs.

Worked example: 12,000 sq ft of board

12,000 x 0.01 = 120 gallons. 120 / 4.5 = 26.7, so 27 pails before waste. On the 9.4 figure it is 112.8 gallons, or 26 pails. The gap between the two products is one pail, which is smaller than the error you will make guessing waste, so decide waste from your own history rather than from a published rule. Neither USG sheet gives one.

What does the finish level change?

A lot, and the manufacturer figure does not split it for you. The Gypsum Association's GA-214 defines the levels, and National Gypsum's summary spells out the coats. At Level 4, tape is embedded in every joint, flat joints get two coats, interior angles get one, and fastener heads get three. Level 5 adds a skim coat over the entire surface, so compound goes on every square foot and not only at the joints.

Per-level coverage figures: not checked. Neither USG sheet publishes them. For a Level 5 skim, ask your supplier's rep for the product's skim coverage or use your own numbers from past jobs.

On the takeoff, the practical fix is to carry finished square feet as separate lines by level. If the spec reads "Level 4 typical, Level 5 at corridors," you have two quantities, not one. See drywall finish levels 0 to 5 for what each level means.

Which square feet do you finish?

Not always the same ones you order. Board square feet for the order count every layer. Compound follows the surface that gets finished, so on a two-layer wall check whether the spec has you finish only the face layer. Ceilings count at the same rate as walls, and both faces of a partition count when both are finished. If a wall type has board on one side only, count one face.

How do you estimate tape and corner bead alongside it?

Tape follows linear feet of joint, which comes from the sheet layout rather than from square feet. As geometry, not a manufacturer figure: each joint in a field of sheets is shared by two of them, so a 4x8 sheet (24 ft of perimeter) carries about 12 LF of joint on average, before wall ends, butt joints and corners change it. Corner bead comes from outside-corner runs in linear feet. The drywall accessories takeoff page covers tape, bead, screws and trim item by item.

Where do the board square feet come from?

From the same takeoff that gives you sheets: wall length by wall type, times height, times faces and layers. A compound number is only as good as that area, and a wall that is missing from the count is missing from the mud order too.

Compound is derived from the board square feet with the method above, so the arithmetic stays in your sheet where you can change the coverage figure or add your own waste.

FAQ

How many pails of joint compound per 1,000 sq ft?

About 2.2 pails of 4.5 gallons at USG's published 10 gallons per 1,000 sq ft, before waste. USG's own FAQ says roughly two pails for its UltraLightweight compound.

How much joint compound does one sheet of drywall take?

About 0.32 gallon for a 4x8 sheet (32 sq ft) at 10 gallons per 1,000 sq ft. That is a planning figure from one manufacturer, not a measured crew rate.

Does Level 5 use more compound than Level 4?

Yes. Level 5 adds a skim coat over the whole surface on top of the Level 4 coats. A per-level coverage figure was not checked, so use your supplier's product data or your own job history for the skim.

Should I add waste to the compound estimate?

The USG coverage sheets do not include a waste factor, so add one from your own history and keep it in its own cell so you can change it per job.
