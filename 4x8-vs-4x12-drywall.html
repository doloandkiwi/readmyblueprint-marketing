4x8 vs 4x12 drywall: which sheet size to order

By The ReadMyBlueprint Team · Nov 4, 2026

Short answer: use 4x12 on walls longer than 8 ft and on ceilings, where it cuts the number of end joints, as long as the crew can get it into the room. Use 4x8 in small rooms, closets, bathrooms and anywhere board is carried up stairs by hand. Many jobs end up ordering both.

A 4x8 sheet covers 32 sq ft and a 4x12 covers 48 sq ft, so two 4x12 sheets equal three 4x8 sheets. The sheet count changes, but the real difference is joints, handling and how the board fits the wall heights on your plans.

What actually changes between the two sizes

  • End (butt) joints. Hung horizontally, a wall longer than the sheet needs an end joint in every course. End joints are the slow ones to finish because sheet ends are not tapered. Longer sheets mean fewer of them.
  • Handling. A 4x12 has half again the area of a 4x8 of the same thickness, so it weighs about half again as much. Ceilings usually mean a lift or two people.
  • Access. Stairwells, hallways, elevators and finished houses decide more orders than the math does. A 12 ft sheet that cannot turn the corner is waste.
  • Fit to the wall. On a wall shorter than the sheet, the 12 ft board gets cut down and the joint savings disappear.

Worked example: one 24 ft wall, 8 ft high

Wall area: 24 x 8 = 192 sq ft, one side, one layer, hung horizontally in two 4 ft courses.
4x12: each course is two 12 ft sheets, one end joint. 2 courses = 4 sheets (192 / 48 = 4.0) and 2 end joints, 4 ft each = 8 LF of end joint.
4x8: each course is three 8 ft sheets, two end joints. 2 courses = 6 sheets (192 / 32 = 6.0) and 4 end joints = 16 LF of end joint.
Same wall, same square feet of board, half the end joints with 4x12. Now change the wall to 14 ft long: 4x12 is 12 + 2 per course, 4x8 is 8 + 6 per course. Both have one end joint per course, so 4x12 saves nothing on joints there and only makes the board harder to carry.

Waste is applied after this, on the total board area per size, then rounded up once. See the drywall waste factor guide for the order of operations.

Wall height decides more than people expect

  • 8 ft walls: two 4 ft courses fit exactly with either size. The choice is only about wall length and access.
  • 9 ft walls: two 4 ft courses leave a 1 ft strip. Many crews order 54 inch wide board instead, which covers 9 ft in two courses. Confirm your supplier stocks it before building the takeoff around it.
  • 10 ft walls: 4x10 hung vertically, or two 4 ft courses plus a 2 ft strip. Taller commercial walls often go vertical with board cut to height.

How to split one order between sizes

Sort walls by length and room before you total anything. A simple rule set: walls over 8 ft long and ceilings in open rooms go to 4x12; closets, bathrooms, short walls and anything behind a tight stair go to 4x8. Total board area for each group, apply waste, divide by 48 or 32, and round each line up once. Keep 5/8 inch Type X and moisture-resistant board as their own lines too, since a 4x12 Type X and a 4x12 standard sheet are different items on the delivery ticket. 4x8 vs 4x12 drywall FAQ

Is 4x8 or 4x12 drywall better?

Neither is better everywhere. 4x12 is usually the better choice on long walls and ceilings where it removes end (butt) joints and you can get it into the room. 4x8 is usually better in small rooms, closets, bathrooms and anywhere the board has to go up stairs or through tight doors by hand.

How many sheets of 4x12 equal one sheet of 4x8?

A 4x8 sheet covers 32 sq ft and a 4x12 covers 48 sq ft, so two 4x12 sheets cover the same area as three 4x8 sheets. Always count from area, then round up per board type.

Does 4x12 drywall mean less taping?

On walls longer than 8 ft, usually yes, because each course has fewer end joints. End joints are the hard ones to finish because the sheet ends are not tapered. On a wall 8 ft or shorter, 4x8 hung horizontally has no end joint at all, so 4x12 saves nothing there.

Is 4x12 drywall harder to hang?

It has 50% more area than a 4x8 of the same thickness, so it weighs about half again as much and is longer to carry. On ceilings most crews use a drywall lift or two people. Check the manufacturer's data sheet for the weight of the exact board you are ordering.

What size drywall should I use for 9 ft walls?

Two 4 ft courses leave a 1 ft strip, so many crews order 54 inch wide board (4.5 ft) for 9 ft walls, which covers the height in two courses with no strip. Check that your supplier stocks it in the thickness and type you need before you base the takeoff on it.

Should I order both 4x8 and 4x12 on one job?

Often, yes. Put 4x12 on long walls and ceilings and 4x8 in closets, bathrooms and small rooms. Keep each size as its own line on the takeoff and round each line up once, so you are not ordering a partial sheet twice.

Does sheet size change the waste factor?

It can. Longer board usually means fewer short offcuts on long runs, and more waste in small rooms where a 12 ft sheet is cut down anyway. Use your own overage history per size if you have it; our drywall waste factor guide covers the starting points.
