How many sheets of drywall for a 2-car garage?

By The ReadMyBlueprint Team · Nov 10, 2026 · Updated Nov 10, 2026

A 24 x 24 ft two-car garage with 8 ft walls and a 16 x 7 ft door takes about 43 sheets of 4x8 drywall for walls and ceiling, or about 30 sheets of 4x12. The walls are 23 sheets of 4x8 and the ceiling is 20. Those numbers include 10 percent for waste and take the garage door out, because 112 sq ft of opening is too big to leave in.

The math, step by step

  • Perimeter: 24 + 24 + 24 + 24 = 96 LF.
  • Wall area: 96 LF x 8 ft = 768 sq ft.
  • Garage door: 16 x 7 = 112 sq ft. Wall area after the door: 656 sq ft.
  • Ceiling area: 24 x 24 = 576 sq ft.
  • Walls in 4x8: 656 / 32 = 20.5, x 1.10 waste = 22.6, round up to 23 sheets.
  • Ceiling in 4x8: 576 / 32 = 18, x 1.10 waste = 19.8, round up to 20 sheets.
  • Walls in 4x12: 656 / 48 = 13.7, x 1.10 = 15.03, round up to 16 sheets. Ceiling in 4x12: 576 / 48 = 12, x 1.10 = 13.2, round up to 14 sheets.

Walls and ceiling are rounded separately because they are often different board. We used 10 percent because a garage is a plain rectangle with long runs. Our drywall waste factor guide explains when to go higher.

Check the wall height before you trust any of this

Garage slabs usually sit lower than the house floor, so a garage wall can be a foot or more taller than the house wall next to it. Take the height from the building sections, not from the house's ceiling height. Going from 8 ft to 10 ft on a 24 x 24 garage adds 7 sheets of 4x8, and that is before you find out the top row needs a strip.

Which garage walls need fire-separation board

This is where a garage stops being a plain room. Table R302.6 of the 2021 IRC (linked as adopted in Virginia, so check what your jurisdiction adopted) sets the minimums for an attached garage:

  • Wall to the house, and separation from attics: at least 1/2 in. gypsum board on the garage side.
  • Ceiling with habitable rooms above: at least 5/8 in. Type X.
  • Structure holding up that floor or ceiling: at least 1/2 in. gypsum board. This is the line that gets missed, because it can put board on walls or beams you had written off as unfinished.
  • Garage under 3 ft from a dwelling on the same lot: at least 1/2 in. on the inside of the exterior walls in that zone.

The same section excepts garage walls running perpendicular to the house wall from the wall separation. Plan notes can go past the code minimum, so read them before you price 1/2 in. on the common wall. Types of drywall covers what each board is for.

If you only board the separation

Take that same 24 x 24 garage with 8 ft walls and one 24 ft wall shared with the house. The common wall is 192 sq ft: 192 / 32 = 6, x 1.10 = 6.6, so 7 sheets. Add the 20-sheet ceiling and the separation-only count is 27 sheets of 4x8, against 43 for a fully boarded garage. If there are rooms overhead, those 20 ceiling sheets are 5/8 in. Type X and go on their own line.

When a house plan does not say how far the garage finish goes, settle it before it goes in the count: the separation wall and ceiling only, or every wall? Sixteen sheets is too much to settle by guessing.

Where garage math breaks on a full plan set

On a full set, the same garage wall shows up on the floor plan, the foundation plan, the elevations and the sections. Count it once, from the floor plan. We learned that one on our own tool: when we ran the 21-page Freeport Square apartment set, walls picked up from ceiling plans, elevations and details pushed the total to 17,648 LF. Counting from the floor plans only brought it to 8,684 LF, with every sheet left out listed by name. That set was apartments, so take it as the kind of mistake to watch for, not a rate that applies to your house plans. We wrote it up in counting walls once.

Common misses: using the house ceiling height on the garage, leaving the overhead door in the wall area, pricing the common wall in 1/2 in. when the plan notes ask for 5/8 in. Type X, and forgetting the board on the structure under rooms above.

Frequently asked questions

How many sheets of drywall do I need for a 2-car garage?

A 24 x 24 ft garage with 8 ft walls and a 16 x 7 ft door takes about 43 sheets of 4x8 for walls and ceiling, or about 30 sheets of 4x12. That includes 10 percent waste.

How many square feet of drywall is a 24x24 garage?

With 8 ft walls, 768 sq ft of wall less 112 sq ft for the garage door is 656 sq ft, plus 576 sq ft of ceiling, 1,232 sq ft in total before waste.

Should I subtract the garage door?

Yes. A 16 x 7 ft door, or two 8 x 7 ft doors, is 112 sq ft of wall with no board on it. Leave standard walk doors and small windows in the count.

Does a garage need 5/8 Type X drywall?

Under the 2021 IRC, Type X at 5/8 inch is required between the garage and habitable rooms above it. The wall to the house and the separation from attics need at least 1/2 inch gypsum board on the garage side. Local amendments and the plan notes can ask for more.

Do I have to drywall the whole garage?

The IRC separation applies to the wall shared with the house, not to every wall, and walls perpendicular to the house are excepted from the wall separation. Whether the other walls get board is a scope question, so check the plans and the contract.
