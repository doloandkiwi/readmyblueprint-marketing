Questions to ask before buying AI takeoff software

By The ReadMyBlueprint Team · Oct 29, 2026 · Updated Oct 29, 2026

Before you buy AI takeoff software, upload one of your own plan sets and check four things: whether it reads your wall-type legend, how it marks walls it is unsure about, what happens on sheets it cannot read, and whether the export drops into your bid sheet. Below are the questions to ask any vendor, and how ReadMyBlueprint answers each one. Your first plan set is free, no card.

Test it on your own plans, free

1. Can I test it on my own plans before I pay?

Ask for this first. A demo on the vendor's cleanest plan set tells you little. Use a set you already bid, so you know the real answer. With ReadMyBlueprint your first plan set is free and needs no card, so run a job you have already priced and compare line by line.

2. Does it read the wall-type legend on my plans?

Every wall is only as good as the legend it is checked against. ReadMyBlueprint reads the wall-type legend drawn on your own plan set and compares each wall's drawn thickness with it. If the set has no legend, walls are still measured, but most come back as needs confirmation. Layout matters too: in our Freeport Square test the legend shared a sheet with the door schedule, and the PDF text interleaved the two sets of columns, so the tool flagged those walls instead of picking a material. See how a wall-type legend reads.

3. How does it flag walls it is not sure about?

Look for a tool that shows uncertainty instead of hiding it. Each ReadMyBlueprint result carries a label: matched (one legend candidate), ambiguous (every candidate shown), or needs confirmation (no match). Nothing gets a silent best guess. Rows it cannot identify stay out of the totals until you confirm them. Details are on confidence labels.

The one number we publish. On one test plan set, Freeport Square (21 pages), 68.9% of walls were auto-matched to a legend type and 99.8% were resolved after one "Confirm all". That is one test set, not a promise for yours.

4. What happens on sheets it cannot read?

It needs a digital (vector) PDF exported from CAD or Revit. A scanned or photographed page has no drawing data, so it says it cannot measure that page instead of estimating. If only some pages are scanned, the digital pages still work. Ceiling plans, elevations and details redraw walls already on the floor plans, so those sheets are set aside to avoid double counting, and you are told which ones.

5. Can I fix a wrong or missing wall myself?

Ask how corrections work. You can confirm flagged walls one at a time or with "Confirm all", with no re-upload, and your confirmation is recorded as yours rather than relabeled as matched. It also has a hand-measure tool and Manual count for anything it does not detect, such as doors, windows and fixtures, which it does not auto-detect.

6. What can I export?

You want numbers that land in your own bid sheet. ReadMyBlueprint exports the takeoff as CSV or Excel, one row per material with item, quantity, unit, count and notes. It can also build a priced PDF or Excel quote from the takeoff. See takeoff to Excel and quote.

7. How long does setup take?

There is nothing to install. It runs in the browser: sign up, upload the PDF, wait for the scan. The 21-page Freeport Square set took about a minute; a 100+ page set can take several minutes. That is one set, and your timing will vary with page count and file.

8. What does it not do?

A vendor that lists no limits is hiding them. ReadMyBlueprint does not read scanned PDFs, does not auto-detect doors, windows, pipe or fixtures, has no live regional cost data, and is assisted takeoff: you verify every bid before it goes out. It covers drywall, metal and wood framing, insulation and CMU, not every trade. We deliver only counts verified against the drawings and list what is missing and why.

A quick checklist to print

  • Run one plan set you have already bid, and compare line by line.
  • Check that it reads your legend, and what it does when the legend is jumbled.
  • Confirm uncertain walls are labeled, not guessed.
  • Confirm unreadable sheets are named, not skipped silently.
  • Open the export in Excel before you commit.
  • Ask for the limits in writing.
