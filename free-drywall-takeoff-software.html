Which free option fits your job

  • One room or a quick check: a drywall calculator. You enter length and height, it returns sheets and mud.
  • A full plan set, one bid: run it through ReadMyBlueprint's free first plan set and compare with your own count.
  • Ongoing manual takeoff: a trial of a click-to-measure tool. It ends, then it's paid.

Most free drywall tools measure; few return the material list

Free measuring tools (a PDF editor's measure tools, open-source tools such as OpenTakeoff, free versions of paid products such as STACK Takeoff and Estimate) give you lengths and areas. Turning those into boards, studs, track and insulation is still your work. FloorFigures is another free tool people cite for residential sheet quantities; we have not checked it, so see their site. ReadMyBlueprint's free first plan set returns the full material list by wall type.

Why "free" usually stops at calculators

A calculator needs you to already know the wall length. On a commercial set that is the hard part: wall types are scattered across floor plans and a legend, and one partition type might be 3-5/8" studs with 5/8" Type X on both sides in one place and a one-sided furring wall in another. A free tool that can't read the legend leaves that sorting to you.

What the free first plan set includes

Your first plan set on ReadMyBlueprint is a full run, not a demo. Each wall is measured from the PDF's own vector geometry and matched to your wall-type legend. You get studs, track, drywall sheets, insulation and CMU block, each traced to its wall, plus a CSV export. Anything it can't match is labeled for you to confirm.

What it can't do

Digital PDFs give draft counts in minutes; scans and photos are counted too, checked within 24 hours. It has no labor rates or regional costs built in. One plan set is free per account, then see pricing. You verify every bid before it goes out.
