Wall assembly calculator

A steel-stud wall needs one stud per spacing interval plus one, two runs of track, and board area equal to net wall area times the layers on each side. Enter your wall types below and get studs, track LF, drywall sheets (4x8, 4x10, 4x12) with waste, and insulation SF, per wall type and in total. Every formula is listed under the calculator.

Method

Formulas and assumptions

  • Field studs = ceil(length x 12 / spacing in) + 1.
  • Opening studs = 2 per opening (jamb pair), times quantity.
  • Corner studs = 1 extra per corner or wall intersection you enter.
  • Total studs = field + opening + corner. All studs are cut at wall height; cripples, king-stud doubling and blocking are not included.
  • Track LF = 2 x length (top and bottom). Doors: bottom track removed (minus width) and header track added (plus width), net zero. Windows: header plus sill, so plus 2 x width.
  • Net wall area = length x height minus the sum of opening width x height x quantity. Every opening is deducted in full.
  • Drywall sheets = net area x total layers (both sides) / sheet area (32, 40 or 48 sq ft) x (1 + waste), rounded up. Per-wall-type rows round up each type; the total row combines unrounded sheet counts before rounding, so it can be lower than the sum of the rows.
  • Insulation SF = net wall area, once, if insulation is selected. No waste is applied.
  • Stud size and gauge are labels used to identify the wall type. Track is assumed to match the stud size. Height and spacing apply per wall type.
  • Not included: fasteners, mud, tape, bead, deflection track, soffits, ceilings.

Worked examples (computed by hand)

Example 1: 20 ft x 10 ft wall, 16 in spacing, 1 door 3x7, 2 corners, 1 layer each side, insulated, 10% waste. Studs: 240/16 = 15, +1 = 16, +2 (door), +2 (corners) = 20. Track: 2 x 20 = 40 LF. Net area: 200 - 21 = 179 sq ft. Board: 179 x 2 = 358 sq ft. 4x8: 358/32 = 11.19 x 1.1 = 12.31, so 13. 4x10: 8.95 x 1.1 = 9.85, so 10. 4x12: 7.46 x 1.1 = 8.20, so 9. Insulation: 179 SF.

Example 2: 33 ft x 9 ft wall, 24 in spacing, 2 windows 4x3 and 1 door 3x7, 1 corner, 2 layers on one side and 1 on the other, no insulation, 10% waste. Studs: 396/24 = 16.5, rounds up to 17, +1 = 18, +6 (3 openings), +1 = 25. Track: 66 + 2 x (2 x 4) = 82 LF. Net area: 297 - 24 - 21 = 252 sq ft. Board: 252 x 3 = 756 sq ft. 4x8: 23.63 x 1.1 = 25.99, so 26. 4x10: 18.9 x 1.1 = 20.79, so 21. 4x12: 15.75 x 1.1 = 17.33, so 18. Insulation: 0.

Example 3: Examples 1 and 2 as two wall types in one run. Studs 20 + 25 = 45. Track 40 + 82 = 122 LF. Board 358 + 756 = 1,114 sq ft. 4x8: 34.81 x 1.1 = 38.29, so 39. 4x10: 27.85 x 1.1 = 30.64, so 31. 4x12: 23.21 x 1.1 = 25.53, so 26 (the per-type rows would sum to 27). Insulation: 179 SF.

Questions

Common questions

How many studs do I need for a wall?

Divide the wall length in inches by the stud spacing, round up, and add one for the end stud. This page then adds 2 studs per opening and 1 per corner or wall intersection. For a 20 ft wall at 16 in on center, 240 / 16 = 15, plus 1 is 16 field studs before openings and corners.

How much track does a metal stud wall need?

One run of top track and one run of bottom track, so 2 x wall length. This calculator removes the bottom track at doors, adds header track over every opening, and adds sill track under windows. Net effect: doors add nothing, each window adds 2 x its width.

How many sheets of drywall for a wall?

Take net wall area (length x height minus openings), multiply by the total number of board layers on both sides, divide by the sheet area (32, 40 or 48 sq ft) and round up after adding waste. The page shows all three sheet sizes at once.

Does the calculator deduct door and window openings?

Yes. Every opening you enter is deducted in full from the board and insulation area. Many estimators ignore small openings because the cut-outs are rarely reusable, so set those to zero quantity if that is your practice.

What waste factor should I use?

The default is 10 percent, which is a common starting point for drywall. Use a higher number for short walls, many corners or soffits. Waste applies to drywall only; insulation and framing are shown without waste.

Can I mix different wall types in one calculation?

Yes. Add a card for each wall type (for example a 3-5/8 in 20 ga partition and a 6 in 18 ga shaft wall), give each its own layers per side, and the page totals them. Stud gauge and size are labels for grouping; they do not change the count.

What does this calculator not include?

Fasteners, joint compound, tape, corner bead, deflection track details, blocking, cripple studs, soffits and bulkheads, and ceilings. It is a quantity estimate to check your own numbers, not a replacement for reading the plans.
