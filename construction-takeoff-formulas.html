Construction takeoff formulas: drywall, studs, track, insulation and CMU

By The ReadMyBlueprint Team · Nov 26, 2026 · Updated Nov 26, 2026

Construction takeoff formulas turn wall length and height into material counts. Drywall sheets = wall area ÷ 32 for 4x8 board. Studs = wall length × 0.75 + 1 at 16 inches on center. Track = wall length × 2. CMU blocks = wall area × 1.125. Then add 5 to 15% waste.

Every example below uses the same wall so you can check the math: 120 linear feet of interior wall, 9 feet high, board on both sides, one straight run.

Drywall sheet formula

Wall area is wall length times height, once per side that gets board. A 4x8 sheet covers 32 square feet and a 4x12 covers 48. Most estimators do not subtract doors and small windows because the offcuts rarely get reused; subtract large openings such as storefronts or overhead doors. Ceilings are the room area divided by the same sheet size. More detail: how to estimate drywall sheets.

Stud and track formula

At 16 inches on center there are 0.75 studs per foot of wall, plus one to close the run. At 24 inches it is 0.5 per foot plus one. The plus-one applies to every separate run, so ten short walls need ten extra studs, not one. Add studs at each corner, intersection and opening (king and jack studs). Track runs along the floor and the ceiling, so it is twice the wall length. Gauge and stud size come from the wall type legend, not the formula. Insulation formula

Insulation goes in the cavity once, so use one side of the wall, not both. Divide the area by the coverage printed on the bag or bundle; coverage changes a lot with R-value and batt width, so never reuse a number from another product. Framing takes up part of the cavity, so gross wall area slightly overstates the need, which is why 5% waste is usually enough.

CMU block formula

A standard 8x16 face block with 3/8 inch mortar joints covers 0.889 square feet, which is 1.125 blocks per square foot of wall. Subtract openings, then add about 5%. Bond beams, lintels and half blocks are counted separately. More: how many CMU blocks.

Where formulas go wrong on a real plan set

  • Wall types. A plan set rarely has one wall. Each type in the legend can change stud size, gauge, spacing, board layers and insulation, so the formula has to run once per wall type with that type's own length.
  • Measuring the length. The formulas are only as good as the linear feet you feed them, and measuring every wall by type is most of the work.
  • Extras. Corners, intersections, openings, blocking, backing, soffits and bulkheads are not in a straight-run formula. Add them yourself.
  • Heights. Walls to deck and walls to ceiling on the same sheet have different heights. Check the sections.

Questions

What is the formula for drywall sheets?

Wall length times height times the number of sides that get board, divided by 32 for a 4x8 sheet or 48 for a 4x12 sheet. Example: 120 LF of wall, 9 ft high, both sides is 2,160 sq ft, which is 68 sheets of 4x8 before waste and 75 with 10%.

How do you calculate studs for a wall?

At 16 inches on center, multiply wall length by 0.75 and add one per run. At 24 inches, multiply by 0.5 and add one. 120 ft of wall at 16 inches is 91 studs before waste. Add studs for corners, intersections and openings.

How much track do I need for metal studs?

Twice the wall length, because track runs along the floor and the ceiling. 120 ft of wall needs 240 ft of track, which is 24 ten-foot sticks before waste.

How many CMU blocks per square foot?

1.125 blocks per square foot for standard 8x16 blocks with 3/8 inch mortar joints. 800 sq ft of wall is 900 blocks, about 945 with 5% waste.

What waste factor should I use in a takeoff?

10 to 15% for drywall, 5 to 10% for studs and track, about 5% for insulation and CMU. Go higher for walls with many corners, openings or short runs.
