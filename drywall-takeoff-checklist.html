Drywall takeoff checklist: what to count besides sheets

Oct 16, 2026 · Guide

Sheet count is the number everyone checks. It is rarely where a drywall bid loses money. The misses are the items that ride along with the board: tape and compound, bead, fasteners, track, shaft wall parts, control joints and the details hidden in sections and specs. This checklist covers what to pull from the plans after the board is counted.

Start with three base quantities

Almost every item below is driven by one of three numbers. Get them right per wall type and the rest is arithmetic.

The board math itself is covered in how to estimate drywall sheets: length × height × sides × layers, minus big openings, plus waste.

1. Finishing materials

  • Joint tape. A common starting point is about 370 to 400 LF of paper tape per 1,000 sq ft of board. Check it against your own jobs.
  • Joint compound. Coverage depends on the product (ready-mix or setting type) and the finish level. Use the manufacturer's coverage chart and your own job history, not one fixed number.
  • Finish level. Read the spec for the level of finish (Level 0 to Level 5 under GA-214). A Level 5 skim-coat area uses far more compound and labor than Level 4. Note where it changes, such as lobbies or walls under critical lighting.
  • Primer, if painting prep is in your scope.

2. Bead and trim

  • Outside corner bead by LF of outside corners, including soffit and bulkhead edges and column wraps.
  • J-bead or L-bead where board stops against another material: window frames, storefront, concrete, ceilings with a reveal.
  • Reveals and specialty trims called out on the details or finish schedule.

3. Fasteners and adhesive

  • Screws. A rough rule is about one screw per square foot of board. Spacing is tighter on ceilings and set by the rated assembly, so check the wall-type detail.
  • Screw length by layer. Two-layer walls need longer screws for the face layer. Do not price the whole job at one length.
  • Laminating adhesive, where an assembly calls for it.

4. Framing and track (if you carry it)

  • Top and bottom track by wall LF, per stud size and gauge.
  • Deflection or slip track at the head of walls that run to the deck.
  • Backing and blocking for cabinets, grab bars, handrails and wall-hung fixtures. These often show only on interior elevations.

Stud counts have their own guide: how to calculate metal studs.

5. Rated, shaft and acoustic walls

  • Shaft wall. Shaftliner panel, CH or CT studs and J-track are a different system from a standard partition. Count them as their own line.
  • Fire caulk and firestopping at the head of rated walls and at penetrations, if it is in your scope.
  • Acoustic sealant and sound batts on STC-rated walls.
  • Resilient channel where an assembly calls for it.

6. Control joints

ASTM C840 sets maximum spacing for control joints in gypsum board, including about 30 ft along a wall run. Long corridors and large ceilings need them even when the drawings do not show each one. Count them by LF and add the labor.

7. Ceilings, soffits and bulkheads

  • Hard-lid ceilings by area from the reflected ceiling plan. See the ceiling drywall takeoff guide.
  • Soffits and bulkheads: the bottom face plus both vertical faces, and bead on every edge.
  • Ceiling framing (main runners, furring channel, hangers) if you carry it.

8. Items found only in the specs

  • Access panels from the mechanical and plumbing drawings.
  • Moisture-resistant, abuse-resistant and cement board in specific rooms. See types of drywall.
  • Temporary protection, cleanup and lifts on tall or open areas.
Tip: keep every line split by wall type. If the base quantities are right per type, a revised drawing changes a few lines instead of the whole bid. The drywall bid sheet guide shows how to turn these lines into a bid, and the free bid-prep toolkit has a template.

Common questions

What should a drywall takeoff include besides sheets?
Finishing materials, bead and trim, fasteners, track and framing if you carry them, shaft wall parts, control joints, head-of-wall details, sealants, backing, access panels and anything in the specs.

How much tape per 1,000 sq ft of drywall?
About 370 to 400 LF is a common starting point. Check it against your own jobs.

Does ReadMyBlueprint count tape, mud and corner bead?
No. It counts board and wall quantities by wall type, and you derive the rest.
