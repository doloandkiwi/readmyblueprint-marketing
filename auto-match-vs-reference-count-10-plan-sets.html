Auto-match vs reference count: what we found on 10 real plan sets

By The ReadMyBlueprint Team · Nov 4, 2026 · Updated Nov 4, 2026

On the 8 real plan sets where we kept an engine summary, the wall-type matcher matched anything on 3 and matched 0% on 5, and 4 of the 8 got no wall length measured at all. The reference counts we built for 9 sets show why: untagged walls, low walls and new-vs-existing scope. Your first plan set is free, no card.

Which walls the matcher missed, and why

  • Tags on a different sheet. On the restaurant remodel the wall types (2A to 2K) were tagged on a separate wall-framing plan. The engine labeled walls by thickness only, such as "unspecified ~5.75 in partition wall" (285 LF) and "unspecified ~7.5 in CMU wall" (731 LF).
  • Low walls. 171.3 of the 276.9 reference LF on the restaurant (62%) were knee walls (110.2 LF, 4 ft to 4 ft 10-1/2 in high) and bar walls (61.1 LF, 3 ft 6 in high). A takeoff that assumes full-height partitions overstates board on those.
  • Untagged walls. The retail set had 195.3 LF of upper roof wall tagged on no plan; the public-building set had 13.4 LF of untagged new wall. Both are listed as assumed.
  • Existing vs new. The engine does not separate them. The restaurant's engine total included 764 LF in CMU-labeled rows, and the reference count has existing CMU as excluded and new CMU as zero.
  • Mixed wood and metal. On the public-building set the engine's 9 LF ambiguity ("3 5/8 in metal stud or 2x4 wood stud") and 7 LF wood match covered 16 LF; the reference count found 42.9 LF of tagged metal-stud walls.

A failure we hit on our own first pass

On the restaurant remodel, our first reference pass used the floor plan and never opened the wall-framing plan that tags every new wall. It listed the restroom interior as 32.9 LF; the tagged mid wall is 13.5 LF, so the first number was 144% high. It priced a restroom ceiling as drywall when the finish schedule says acoustical tile. And about 200 LF of new framed walls sat in "could not confirm" even though the tags answered them. The third pass settled at 276.9 LF confirmed, with parapet, east stair walls and a patio ceiling left as scope questions.

How much repeat counts of one plan set vary

  • Entertainment venue (set E): four passes gave 1,085, 955, 1,049 and 974 LF gross. The 1,085 double-counted box outlines drawn with an inner and outer line; the 1,049 included 178 ft of dashed connector lines between separate walls. Reconciled: 978 LF. Pass 2 was 2.4% from the delivered number and pass 4 was 0.4%.
  • Retail building (set G): three passes on one basis (new above-grade walls) gave 957.7, 962.3 and 989 LF, within 3.3%. The interior wood walls were over 10% apart across passes (301, 274.5 and 364 LF), so a fourth vector pass settled them. The final 1,144.6 LF is a wider basis than those three passes.
  • High-rise fit-out (set J): four passes on level 16 gave 121 to 134 m (125 m used) and on level 17, 92 to 115 m (95 m used). An extra wall-by-wall pass moved the steel-stud partition total from 213 m to 220 m (+3.3%).

What this means before you bid

A matcher can measure a wall and still not know what it is, whether it is new, or how tall it is. That is why ReadMyBlueprint labels each result matched, ambiguous or needs confirmation instead of guessing, and why a person reads the partition sheets before a bid goes out. For the one set we verified end to end, see the Freeport Square test: 68.9% of walls auto-matched and 99.8% after one "Confirm all." For public bid sets, see 9 public bid sets. Methodology and limits

  • Sample: 10 plan sets sent to us or uploaded from Oct 22 to Nov 4, 2026. Two sets (I, J) have no engine summary on file; one (H) has no reference count. Two other requests in the same folder were not plan sets and are excluded.
  • Anonymized: no names, emails, addresses or project names. Project types are generic. Only aggregate lengths and counts are published; no plan sheet is shown.
  • Engine figures are the matcher's summary for each upload at that time. Engine versions changed during these weeks, so rows are not one build.
  • Reference counts are our own work. They come from independent vector measurement passes of the PDF linework, reconciled when passes disagreed. They are not a field-verified estimator takeoff, and none was compared to a customer's own takeoff in this dataset.
  • Engine LF and reference LF are not the same scope. The engine reports every wall-like line it measures; the reference count is new work only. The gap is not an error rate, so no accuracy figure is given.
  • Selection bias: these sets came from people who sent plans, many in response to our outreach. They are not a random sample of the market, and 8 sets is too few to average.
  • Not checked: sheet counts per set (only skipped sheets are logged), time per count, and how often customers' own numbers matched ours.

FAQ

How often does auto-match find the wall types on real plan sets?

On the 8 plan sets where we kept an engine summary, 3 matched any wall length to a legend (7%, 46% and 73% of measured wall length) and 5 matched 0%. Four of the 8 measured no wall length at all. Eight sets is a small sample, not a benchmark, and the spread is the finding.

Why is the engine's total wall length sometimes far above the reference count?

The engine measures every wall-like pair of lines it finds, new and existing. The reference count is new work only. On the restaurant remodel the engine reported 1,518 LF and the reference count was 276.9 LF; half of the engine's length (764 LF) sat in rows labeled as CMU walls, while the reference count lists the CMU shell as existing and new CMU as zero.

How much do repeated counts of the same plan set vary?

On the indoor entertainment venue, four independent passes gave 955 to 1,085 LF gross and reconciled to 978 LF. On the two-story retail building, three passes on a common basis were within 3.3% of each other (957.7 to 989 LF). On the high-rise fit-out, four passes on one level ranged 121 to 134 m. One repeat pass moved a total by 3.3%.

Which walls are hardest to count?

Walls with no type tag, walls shorter than full height such as knee and bar walls, and walls whose build is not shown. In the reference counts, 29.3% of the retail set, 70.7% of the venue set and 23.8% of the public-building set (by LF) were counted as assumed rather than confirmed from the drawings.
