READMYBLUEPRINT — PRE-BID REVIEW CHECKLIST
A practical last pass before a material takeoff goes into a real bid.

1. SCALE CHECK
   Does the sheet's printed scale (e.g. 1/8" = 1'-0") match what your
   takeoff tool actually calibrated to? A wrong scale is the single
   biggest source of a bad count, and it's silent unless you check it
   against the title block yourself.

2. CONFIRM EVERY FLAGGED ITEM
   Anything labeled "ambiguous" or "needs confirmation" should be opened
   back up against the real drawing before it goes into your total —
   don't let a flagged item quietly round to zero or get skipped.

3. WALL HEIGHT
   Drywall, insulation, and CMU block counts all depend on a wall/ceiling
   height that a floor plan often doesn't print directly. Confirm the
   height you used against a section or elevation sheet, not a guess.

4. UNITS MATCH YOUR ESTIMATE
   LF (linear feet) vs. SF (square feet) vs. EA (each) — a mismatch here
   is an easy way to be off by an order of magnitude without any single
   number looking obviously wrong.

5. WASTE / OVERAGE FACTOR
   A raw material count is not a purchase order. Add your own standard
   waste percentage (commonly 5-10%, higher for irregular layouts)
   before pricing — this tool measures the drawing, not your job-site
   cut waste.

6. TRADES NOT COVERED
   Confirm what's actually being auto-counted (steel/wood framing,
   drywall, plywood, insulation, CMU) versus what still needs a manual
   count — electrical, plumbing, mechanical, finishes, and site work
   are not in scope and need their own line items.

7. ADDENDA AND REVISIONS
   Check the sheet's own revision block and any bid addenda issued after
   the original plan set — a takeoff run on an outdated sheet revision
   will confidently produce the wrong number.

8. LEGEND CROSS-CHECK
   For anything wall-type-related, confirm the legend or schedule you're
   reading from is the current one — some projects carry more than one
   wall-type schedule across different sheet sets.

9. SPOT-CHECK BY HAND
   Pick 2-3 walls or areas at random and measure them yourself, by hand,
   against the drawing. If your tool's count is close, that's a good
   sign; if it's off, better to find out on 3 walls than on the whole bid.

10. FINAL GUT CHECK
    Does the total square footage implied by your counts roughly match
    the building's stated gross floor area? A takeoff that implies a
    50,000 SF building on a 20,000 SF floor plan is worth a second look
    before it goes out the door.
