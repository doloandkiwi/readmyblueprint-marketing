Ceiling drywall takeoff: RCPs, soffits and bulkheads

Oct 20, 2026 · Guide

Most drywall takeoff guides stop at the walls. Ceilings are where a lot of board, bead and labor hides, and they come off a different sheet: the reflected ceiling plan (RCP). This guide covers how to read the RCP for gypsum board, how to measure hard-lid ceilings, soffits and bulkheads, and what to carry with them.

1. Read the RCP legend first

Every RCP has a legend or ceiling schedule that says what each hatch or tag means. Find the ones that are gypsum board. They are usually labeled GWB, GYP BD or "hard lid." Acoustic tile (ACT), open-to-structure and specialty ceilings are someone else's scope unless your contract says otherwise.

  • Ceiling type and board. Type X, moisture-resistant or a rated assembly changes the board and the screws. The types of drywall guide covers the differences.
  • Height tags. Each area carries a height (for example 8'-6" AFF). Heights drive lift rental, labor rates and where soffit drops occur.
  • Detail callouts. Bulkheads and soffits point to a section or detail. You need that detail for the drop and width.

Not sure how scales, tags and callouts work? Start with how to read blueprints.

2. Measure hard-lid ceiling area

Measure each gypsum board area at the sheet's scale and keep one line per ceiling type and height. Area is length × width for simple rooms. Break odd shapes into rectangles. Do not deduct light fixtures, diffusers or access panels. They are small, and the board is cut out on site.

Board count follows the same math as walls: area ÷ sheet area, plus waste. Ceilings often use longer sheets to cut down on butt joints, so check what length you plan to hang. The drywall sheet estimating guide walks through the arithmetic.

3. Soffits and bulkheads

This is where ceiling takeoffs most often come up short. A soffit or bulkhead has more board than its footprint on the RCP suggests.

  • Run length from the RCP, per bulkhead.
  • Drop and width from the section or detail the RCP points to.
  • Board area = run × (bottom width + each exposed drop). A bulkhead between two rooms has two drops.
  • Outside corner bead on every exposed edge, by LF. If a soffit meets a wall or window frame, check for J-bead.
  • Framing, if you carry it: track, short studs or furring, and hangers.

4. Ceiling framing (if it is your scope)

  • Suspended drywall grid or main runners and furring channel by area, per the manufacturer's layout.
  • Hanger wire and anchors at the spacing the assembly or spec requires.
  • Perimeter track or angle by LF of ceiling edge.
  • Rated ceilings. A fire-rated ceiling assembly sets framing spacing and fastening. Check the listed assembly before you price.

5. Things that get missed

  • Height changes. Where two ceiling heights meet there is a vertical face of board, even without a detail tag.
  • Control joints. Large gypsum board ceilings need control joints. Count them by LF.
  • Access panels from the mechanical and plumbing sheets.
  • Finish level. Ceilings under raking light may be specified at a higher finish level, which means more compound and labor.
  • Exterior soffits may need exterior-rated board. Check the spec.

The full list of extras that ride along with board is in the drywall takeoff checklist.

Tip: keep ceilings, soffits and walls on separate lines. Walls come from the floor plans and ceilings come from the RCP. If you mix them, a revised RCP means re-checking the whole bid instead of a few lines.

Common questions

How do you take off a drywall ceiling from a reflected ceiling plan?
Find the gypsum board areas in the RCP legend, measure each area at scale, note its height, then add soffit and bulkhead faces and edge bead as separate lines.

How do you calculate drywall for a soffit or bulkhead?
Run length from the RCP, times the bottom width plus each exposed drop from the detail. Bead every exposed edge.

Does ReadMyBlueprint take off ceilings?
