How to estimate CMU block, grout and rebar from drawings

By The ReadMyBlueprint Team · Nov 5, 2026 · Updated Nov 5, 2026

Block comes from net wall area at about 119 units per 100 square feet with waste. Grout comes from counting every core you fill, then dividing by a bag yield. Rebar comes from bar spacing, wall height and splice allowance on the structural sheets. Lintel block comes from each opening width plus bearing at both ends.

Which sheets do I need besides the floor plan?

The floor plan gives wall length. The wall sections give height and show where bond beams sit. The structural notes or masonry schedule give bar size, bar spacing and which cells are grouted. Without those, you can count block but not grout or steel. If the notes say 8 inch CMU with #5 bars at 48 inches on center, that one line drives the whole reinforcement and grout count below.

How many block do I need?

Use net wall area. The National Concrete Masonry Association's note on estimating materials gives a standard 8 x 16 inch unit as exposing 8/9 sq ft of wall, which is 119 units per 100 sq ft of wall with 5 percent waste (TEK 04-02A, Estimating Concrete Masonry Materials, now hosted by CMHA). Our block count guide walks through the arithmetic.

Assumed numbers, not from a real job: one wall, 100 ft long and 10 ft high, 8 inch block, with a 3 ft by 7 ft door (21 sq ft) and a 4 ft by 4 ft window (16 sq ft). Net area is 1,000 - 37 = 963 sq ft, and 963 / 100 x 119 = 1,146 block.

How do I estimate grout?

Count the cores you fill, then convert to bags. Every grouted cell in a reinforced wall fills one core per course for the full height of the bar, a bond beam course fills every core in the course, and lintel courses fill over the opening.

For the bag yield we used a manufacturer's data sheet, not the TEK tables: one 80 lb bag of Quikrete Core-Fill Grout (Fine) yields about 0.68 cubic feet and fills the cores of approximately 3 standard 8 x 8 x 16 block (product data sheet 1585-08). A standard block has two cores, so we treat one bag as about 6 core fills. Yields differ by product, so replace it with the bag you buy. TEK 04-02A also gives grout volumes per wall area and bag yields in Tables 3 to 5, but those tables are images on the page, so we did not copy numbers from them; use them as a cross-check.

Worked example, continuing the same assumed wall: vertical #5 bars at 48 in. o.c. (26 bars on a 100 ft wall), one bond beam course at the top, and 15 courses in 10 ft (15 x 8 in. = 120 in.). Door jamb and window jamb grouting is left out to keep the arithmetic short; add it on a real job.

How do I estimate rebar?

Rebar is a length count. Vertical bars are the wall height plus an allowance for the dowel or lap, once per bar location. Horizontal bars run the length of each bond beam plus lap splices. In the example, with an assumed 2 ft dowel and lap, each vertical bar is 12 ft:

Vertical bars26 x 12 ft312
Bond beam bars2 bars x 100 ft, plus 10 percent assumed for laps220
Lintel bars2 bars each; door 36 in. + 2 x 24 in. extension = 7 ft; window 48 in. + 2 x 24 in. = 8 ft30

The 2 ft lap, the 10 percent, the 24 inch bar extension past each lintel end and the 8 inch lintel bearing are all assumptions for the example. The structural notes set the real splice length and bearing, and they usually set them by bar size. Convert the linear feet to stock lengths and weight with your supplier's list.

How do I count lintel block?

Each opening needs a lintel course as long as the opening plus bearing at both ends, in block. Using an assumed 8 inches of bearing per side, the door is 36 + 16 = 52 in., which is 52 / 16 = 3.25, so 4 lintel block. The window is 48 + 16 = 64 in., exactly 4 block. That gives the 8 lintel block used in the grout table. Bearing length is a drawing detail, so read it from the lintel schedule or the detail sheet.

Frequently asked questions

How many CMU blocks per 100 square feet of wall?

For standard 8 x 16 inch units, NCMA TEK 04-02A gives 119 units per 100 square feet of wall including 5 percent waste. Use net wall area after deducting openings.

How do I estimate grout for a CMU wall?

Count every core you will fill: each grouted vertical cell for the full bar height, every core in bond beam courses, and lintel courses. Divide by the bag yield from your product data sheet, which varies by product, and add spill and waste.

How much grout does one bag fill?

One manufacturer data sheet for an 80 lb bag of core-fill grout states a yield of about 0.68 cubic feet, enough for the cores of roughly 3 standard 8 x 8 x 16 block. Check the data sheet for the product you buy.

How do I estimate rebar from CMU drawings?

Read bar size and spacing from the structural notes. Vertical bars equal wall height plus dowel or lap allowance at each bar location; horizontal bars equal bond beam length plus laps; add lintel bars per opening. Sum the linear feet and convert to stock lengths.

Does ReadMyBlueprint calculate grout and rebar?

No. It measures CMU wall length and area and returns block counts. Mortar, grout and reinforcement are not calculated, and bond beam and lintel block are only broken out when the plan labels them distinctly.
