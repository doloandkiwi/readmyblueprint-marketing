How many sheets of drywall for a 1,000 sq ft house?

By The ReadMyBlueprint Team · Nov 17, 2026 · Updated Nov 17, 2026

A typical single-story 1,000 sq ft house with 8 ft ceilings needs about 156 sheets of 4x8 drywall, or about 104 sheets of 4x12, for all walls and ceilings. That includes 12 percent waste, and the real number usually falls between 128 and 184 sheets of 4x8 depending on how many interior walls the floor plan has.

The assumptions behind the number

Floor area alone does not tell you how much board a house takes. Wall length does. Here is the house we used; swap in your own numbers:

  • One story, 25 x 40 ft footprint, 8 ft ceilings.
  • Exterior perimeter: 25 + 40 + 25 + 40 = 130 LF.
  • Interior partitions: about 150 LF, typical for two or three bedrooms, one or two baths, and a hall.
  • Garage not included. It is a separate line (see below).

The math, step by step

  • Exterior walls, inside face only: 130 LF x 8 ft = 1,040 sq ft.
  • Interior walls, both faces: 150 LF x 8 ft x 2 = 2,400 sq ft.
  • Ceilings: 1,000 sq ft.
  • Total board: 1,040 + 2,400 + 1,000 = 4,440 sq ft.
  • 4x8 sheets: 4,440 / 32 = 138.75, x 1.12 waste = 155.4, round up to 156 sheets.
  • 4x12 sheets: 4,440 / 48 = 92.5, x 1.12 waste = 103.6, round up to 104 sheets.

Walls only: 3,440 sq ft, about 121 sheets of 4x8. Ceilings only: 1,000 sq ft, about 35 sheets of 4x8 or 24 of 4x12.

Interior wall length moves the count most

Two houses with the same 1,000 sq ft can be 50 sheets apart. Every foot of interior partition takes board on both faces, so it counts twice:

  • Open plan, about 100 LF of interior wall: 3,640 sq ft of board, about 128 sheets of 4x8.
  • Typical, about 150 LF: 4,440 sq ft, about 156 sheets.
  • Chopped up, about 200 LF (more bedrooms, closets, a second bath): 5,240 sq ft, about 184 sheets.

If you have the floor plan, add up the partition lengths before anything else. Closets are where people come up short: each one adds three short walls with board on both sides.

If the ceilings are 9 ft

Exterior: 130 x 9 = 1,170 sq ft. Interior: 150 x 9 x 2 = 2,700 sq ft. Plus 1,000 of ceiling = 4,870 sq ft, about 171 sheets of 4x8. Many hangers use 54 in. board on 9 ft walls so two rows fill the height without a 1 ft strip. Vaulted or tray ceilings need their own measurement; the flat floor area undercounts them.

Why exterior walls count once

This is the most common way a whole-house count comes out high. The outside face of an exterior wall gets sheathing and siding, not drywall, so only the inside face is boarded. We hit this on our own test set: on the 21-page Freeport Square apartment plans, 628 LF of 2x6 exterior wall came to 120 sheets once it was counted as one-sided. Treating it like an interior partition would have added roughly that many sheets again.

The same set showed the other big miss on a full plan set: the same wall drawn on the floor plan, the ceiling plan and an enlarged plan gets counted two or three times. Before we fixed it, Freeport read 17,648 LF of wall; after, 8,684 LF. That write-up is in counting walls once.

Things the 1,000 sq ft does not cover

  • Garage: the wall shared with the house and any ceiling under living space usually need fire-rated board. Our 2-car garage guide works that one out.
  • Wet areas: tub and shower walls get cement board or moisture-resistant board, not standard 1/2 in. Count them as their own line. See types of drywall.
  • Ceiling board: 5/8 in. or sag-resistant 1/2 in. is common on ceilings, so ceilings are usually a separate line from walls.
  • Waste: 12 percent suits a house with many small rooms. Long, open walls can run closer to 10 percent. More in the drywall waste factor guide.
Common misses: boarding both faces of exterior walls, forgetting closets, counting a wall twice because it shows up on two sheets, leaving the ceilings out, and adding waste twice.

Frequently asked questions

How many sheets of drywall do I need for a 1,000 sq ft house?

About 156 sheets of 4x8 or 104 sheets of 4x12 for walls and ceilings in a typical single-story 1,000 sq ft house with 8 ft ceilings, including 12 percent waste. Depending on how many interior walls the layout has, expect roughly 128 to 184 sheets of 4x8.

How many square feet of drywall is a 1,000 sq ft house?

About 4,440 sq ft of board before waste for a typical layout: 1,040 sq ft on the inside face of the exterior walls, 2,400 sq ft on both faces of the interior walls, and 1,000 sq ft of ceiling.

Is there a quick rule of thumb?

Board area usually lands around 3.5 to 5 times the floor area for a house with 8 ft ceilings. Use it to sanity-check a count, not to order from. The interior wall length is what moves the number most.

Does the garage count?

Not in the 1,000 sq ft. Garages are measured separately and often need fire-rated board on the house-side wall and the ceiling below living space. Count the garage as its own line.
