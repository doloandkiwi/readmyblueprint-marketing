● About

A takeoff tool built around one rule.

ReadMyBlueprint exists because "AI takeoff" tools kept giving contractors a confident-looking number with no way to check it. Every decision in this product — what it measures, what it refuses to guess at, how results are labeled — comes back to a single rule: never present an estimate as if it were an exact measurement.

Why this exists

A bid is only as good as the numbers under it.

Manual takeoff works — it's just slow, and slow means fewer bids out the door. The "AI takeoff" tools built to fix that mostly solved the speed problem and quietly introduced a trust problem: a number with no way to tell whether it came from a real measurement or a plausible-sounding guess.

ReadMyBlueprint is built the other way around. It starts from a real, calibrated measurement of your actual drawing — never a description of what a wall "probably" is — and only ever adds automation on top of that real geometry: reading your sheet's own printed scale, cross-referencing the wall-type legend your architect already drew, matching a measured thickness against a spec actually present in your document. When it can't do that with real evidence, it says so, in plain language, instead of picking the most likely-looking answer and moving on.

That's assisted takeoff, not automatic takeoff. You still verify every bid before it goes out — the tool's job is to make sure your two minutes of double-checking go to the three walls that actually need it, not all three hundred.

How it's built

Engineering rigor instead of a résumé.

ReadMyBlueprint isn't built by a large estimating team with decades of field experience — it's built by an engineer, and it earns your trust the way engineering earns trust: by testing every measurement approach against real, hand-calculated values on real project plan sets before it ships, not by asking you to take a claimed background on faith.

Concretely: every material-matching rule in this tool has been checked against plan sets from real, multi-hundred-page commercial projects, with the resulting counts hand-verified against what a human estimator would measure by hand. When that testing found a real bug — a coordinate-transform error on rotated sheets, a false-positive coverage warning on thin architectural line weights — it got fixed and re-verified before it shipped, and the fix is documented, not quietly patched over.

Who it's for

The same tool, for very different jobs.

ReadMyBlueprint is used at both ends of the same spectrum on purpose: a homeowner running one small remodel who's never opened an estimating tool before, and a solo estimator or small shop running dozens of sheets a week who just wants the busywork gone. Neither one needs a CAD background or an onboarding call — upload a plan set, and the in-app guide walks you through the rest.

The one rule, in full

If a number can be computed from real vector geometry and a calibrated scale, it's shown as exact. If it can't — a scanned page, no calibration, no matching spec anywhere in the document — the tool says so and asks you to confirm or measure it yourself. It never silently guesses. Every feature in this product is built to keep that true, including the ones that would be easier to ship without it.
