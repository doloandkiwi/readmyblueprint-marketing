How many studs per linear foot for a 2x6 exterior wall?

By The ReadMyBlueprint Team · Nov 5, 2026 · Updated Nov 5, 2026

A 2x6 exterior wall at 16 inches on center takes 0.75 studs per linear foot, and at 24 inches on center it takes 0.5, plus one stud to close the run. Corners and openings add more. In the 100 ft example below, with assumed corners and openings, the real figures came out near 0.96 and 0.71.

What is the quick table?

The base rate is 12 divided by the spacing in inches. That is the studs-per-foot you get from the layout alone.

SpacingLayout studs per LFFor a 100 ft run (layout + 1)
12 in. o.c.1.00101
16 in. o.c.0.7576
24 in. o.c.0.5051

The spacing does not change with stud depth. A 2x6 at 16 inches uses the same number of studs as a 2x4 at 16 inches; the 2x6 only changes the lumber size, the plate size and the insulation that fits in the cavity.

Why is a real wall higher than 0.75 per foot?

Because the layout formula ignores everything except the straight run. A real exterior wall adds studs at every corner, at every intersecting wall, and around every opening. A long wall with few openings stays near the base rate. A short wall with a window every 6 feet does not.

Worked example, with assumed numbers: one exterior wall run of 100 ft with 2 corners (assumed 2 extra studs each) and 4 openings (assumed 2 kings and 2 jacks each).

SpacingLayout + 1CornersOpeningsTotalPer LF
16 in. o.c.76416960.96
24 in. o.c.51416710.71

Notice that moving from 16 to 24 inches saved 25 studs, not the 50 you would expect from the base rate, because the corner and opening studs do not shrink with spacing. If you quote a per-foot number to a customer, quote it for the wall you are bidding, not the base rate.

When can a 2x6 wall be framed at 24 inches?

Only when the code tables and the drawings both allow it. The American Wood Council notes that exterior walls are normally framed at 16 inches, with 24 inches allowed for 2x4 studs in one-story buildings when the sheathing can span between studs, and that the bottom story of a three-story building needs 2x6 or 3x4 studs at no more than 16 inches (AWC, lumber studs). For stud height and spacing by load, the conventional-construction table is IBC Table 2308.5.1, reproduced on UpCodes; the page we opened rendered the table poorly, so read the row for your stud size in the adopted code, not from a summary. The deciding document is your wall-type legend, because the engineer has already picked the number.

Which spacing do the drawings actually say?

Look for a tag like 2x6 at 16 in. o.c. in the wall-type legend or the wall section, not in the general notes. The exterior wall and the interior partitions are usually different types with different spacing, so they are two lines in the takeoff. If the legend text is scrambled in the PDF, the spacing may be readable on the section but not on the legend; we ran into exactly that and wrote it up in when the legend is missing or scrambled. Our legend guide shows what a good one looks like.

What else changes with 2x6 walls?

The cavity is 5-1/2 inches deep, so a 2x6 exterior wall is where R-19 batts show up on legends (our insulation takeoff page shows R-19 batt, 2x6 wall at 16 inches o.c. as a matched example). The plates are 2x6 as well, so the plate footage goes on a 2x6 line, not the 2x4 one.

ReadMyBlueprint reads the spacing from your legend and returns the stud count for each wall type, so the per-foot rate is an output you can check rather than an assumption. How corner and opening extras show up on your set is not something we have checked, so keep them in your own notes. See how we count for what each line shows.

Frequently asked questions

How many studs per linear foot for a 2x6 wall at 16 inches on center?

The layout alone is 0.75 studs per linear foot (12 divided by 16), plus one stud to close the wall. Corners, intersections and openings add more, so a real wall is higher than 0.75 per foot.

How many studs per linear foot at 24 inches on center?

The layout is 0.5 studs per linear foot (12 divided by 24), plus one to close the run. The same corner and opening studs still apply, so the saving over 16 inches is smaller than half.

Does a 2x6 wall use more studs than a 2x4 wall?

No. Stud count depends on spacing, not depth. At the same spacing a 2x6 and a 2x4 wall use the same number of studs; the 2x6 wall costs more in lumber and holds a deeper insulation cavity.

Can I frame a 2x6 exterior wall at 24 inches on center?

Sometimes. The American Wood Council notes 24 inches is allowed for 2x4 studs in one-story buildings with adequate sheathing, and the bottom story of a three-story building needs 2x6 at no more than 16 inches. Your wall-type legend and the adopted code decide.

How do I get studs per foot from my own plans?

Take the total stud count for a wall type and divide by that wall type's linear feet. Software that lists counts per wall type gives you the rate directly; by hand, use length divided by spacing plus one, then add corners and openings.
