Multifamily takeoff software for drywall and framing

Oct 27, 2026 · Apartments, condos and mixed-use

An apartment job is mostly the same few walls drawn many times: demising walls between units, corridor walls, shaft and stair walls, and unit partitions. The work is not hard. It is long, and one wall type mixed up with its look-alike throws off board, studs and insulation across every floor. This page covers what a multifamily drywall and framing takeoff actually needs from software, and where ReadMyBlueprint fits.

What makes a multifamily takeoff different

  • Look-alike wall types. A rated demising wall and a non-rated partition can be drawn the same thickness. In the Freeport Square apartment set we tested, two wall types share a thickness and only one needs fire-rated board. Thickness alone will not tell them apart. The wall-type legend guide shows how to read the difference.
  • Sheets that redraw the same walls. Ceiling plans, enlarged unit plans, elevations and details all show walls that are already on the floor plans. Count them twice and the job looks twice as big.
  • Repetition. A few unit types and typical floors repeat. The method is to take off each unique layout once and multiply, then adjust for end units, accessible units and non-typical floors.
  • Long legends. Apartment sets often carry dozens of wall types. Freeport Square has 25. Each needs its own studs, board layers and insulation.

The step-by-step hand method, including unit repetition and rated walls, is in our apartment building takeoff walkthrough.

What to look for in takeoff software for multifamily

  • Counts by wall type, not just total footage. You price demising, corridor and shaft walls differently, so the output has to keep them apart.
  • Every number traced to a wall and a sheet. On a long job, you need to check a quantity without re-measuring the floor.
  • Honest flags instead of guesses. When the tool can't confirm a wall's spec, it should say so, not fill in a likely-looking stud gauge.
  • A clear list of skipped sheets, so a reviewer can see what was left out and why.
  • An export you can work with. Quantities go into your own bid sheet and pricing.

How ReadMyBlueprint handles an apartment plan set

You upload a digital PDF plan set. ReadMyBlueprint reads the wall-type legend, matches each wall on the floor plans to its type, and returns linear feet, wall area, studs, track, drywall sheets and insulation per wall type, with the sheet each wall came from. Reflected ceiling plans and other sheets that redraw floor-plan walls are set aside and listed, so walls are counted once.

Every wall carries a confidence label. Walls the legend confirms are matched. Walls it can't confirm are flagged for you, not guessed. One "Confirm all" click resolves the short list that is left, and you can review any wall before it counts. If a sheet's legend can't be read cleanly, you can import the wall-type schedule from Excel instead of confirming walls one by one.

Check how your set draws typical floors. If a level is drawn once and noted as typical for several floors, multiply those wall quantities by the floor count in your bid sheet.

The real numbers: on Freeport Square, a 21-page apartment plan set and the one set we have fully verified, 68.9% of walls were auto-matched and 99.8% resolved after "Confirm all". That is one plan set, not a general accuracy figure. Sets that draw wall types as graphic details instead of a text legend will need more confirming by hand. The full write-up is here, and the method is on the accuracy page.

Which trades it covers

  • Drywall: board by wall type and layer count, so rated walls stay separate.
  • Metal framing and wood framing: studs and track from the legend's stud size.
  • Insulation: cavity coverage for demising and exterior walls.
  • CMU: block counts from wall area, for stair and elevator cores drawn as masonry.

To be clear about scope: ReadMyBlueprint takes off walls. Ceilings, soffits and bulkheads come off the reflected ceiling plan by hand. The ceiling takeoff guide covers that part.

Common questions

Can takeoff software tell rated and non-rated walls apart?
Only if it reads the wall-type legend. Two walls of the same thickness can be different assemblies. ReadMyBlueprint matches walls by legend type and flags any it can't confirm.

Does ReadMyBlueprint count walls twice on ceiling plans and enlarged plans?
It sets aside sheets that redraw floor-plan walls, like reflected ceiling plans, and lists which sheets it skipped.

How accurate is it on apartment plan sets?
On the one apartment set we have fully verified, Freeport Square, 68.9% of walls auto-matched and 99.8% after "Confirm all". Other sets will differ, and you verify every bid before it goes out.
