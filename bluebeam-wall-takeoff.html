Bluebeam wall takeoff: how to measure walls in Revu and get to materials

By The ReadMyBlueprint Team · Nov 24, 2026 · Updated Nov 24, 2026

A Bluebeam wall takeoff in Revu means calibrating each page, tracing wall runs with a length measurement, counting openings, and exporting the Markups List to CSV. What comes out is lengths, areas and counts. Studs, track and sheets come from formulas you apply to those totals afterward, one wall type at a time.

Bluebeam Revu is widely used for PDF markup and review, and its measurement tools work on any PDF, scanned or digital, because you do the tracing. This guide covers the steps and where the hours go, so you can judge whether it fits your bids.

What does Revu give you for a wall takeoff?

Bluebeam's own Measurements & Takeoffs course outline lists the pieces: calibration, length, area, perimeter and volume measurements, VisualSearch with count measurements, adding depth to get wall area, custom columns in the Markups List, and export of takeoff data to CSV. That is measurement and organization. It is not a material list, so the conversion to stud, track and board counts is your own spreadsheet.

How do you run a wall takeoff in Bluebeam, step by step?

  1. Calibrate each page. Use the Calibrate option in the Measurements panel: pick two points of known length and enter the real distance. Then measure a second known dimension and compare. If it disagrees, fix the calibration before you trace anything. Scanned sets can change scale from sheet to sheet, so check every page.
  2. Set up one markup per wall type. Save a measurement markup into a custom tool set for each wall type in the legend, and name its subject with the wall tag (B-4, W3 and so on). The tag is what lets you sort the export by wall type later.
  3. Trace wall runs. Use a length measurement along each wall. The course outline lists "adding depth to get wall area," so a run measured with the wall height gives square feet as well as linear feet.
  4. Count openings. Use a count markup per door or window type. VisualSearch can find repeated symbols across a page so you are not clicking each one.
  5. Export from the Markups List. Turn on the measurement columns, add any custom columns, filter, then choose Summary and pick CSV. Bluebeam's support page notes that Revu 21 Basics subscriptions can export only PDF, so check what your tier allows.
  6. Convert in a spreadsheet. Sort by wall tag, look up each type's spacing and layers, and apply the formulas. The framing takeoff in Excel page has them, and the free template has them filled in.

Where do the hours go in a Revu wall takeoff?

  • One click per corner. Every wall run is traced by you, so a missed segment is a missed quantity.
  • The legend match is manual. Revu measures a line. It does not know that tag B-4 means 6 in. studs with two layers of Type X. You carry that from the legend into your tool names and your spreadsheet.
  • Repeated layouts. Copying markups between identical units saves clicks, but only where the units really are identical, and each placement still needs checking.
  • Revisions. If an addendum moves a partition, the affected runs are re-measured by hand.

None of this is a flaw in a markup tool. It is the cost of general-purpose measuring on a wall-heavy trade.

When does Bluebeam alone fit?

It fits well when the plan set is small, when you bid several trades with one tool, when your team already reviews and marks up drawings in Revu, or when you are a GC checking a sub's numbers. A tenant improvement with a handful of partitions is a reasonable case. A fair test for your own work: time one floor plan from first click to a labeled material list, then decide which steps you want to keep doing by hand. FAQ

Can Bluebeam count studs automatically?

Revu measures lengths, areas and counts that you place or search for. Stud counts come from a formula you apply to the wall lengths: length x 12 / spacing, rounded up, plus 1.

How do I get Bluebeam measurements into Excel?

Open the Markups List, choose Summary, and pick CSV. Bluebeam notes that Revu 21 Basics subscriptions can export only PDF, so check your tier.

Do I need to calibrate every page in Revu?

Calibrate every page whose scale differs, and always check with a second known dimension. Scanned sheets in one set can have different scales.

Does ReadMyBlueprint replace Bluebeam?

No. Bluebeam is a general PDF markup and review tool. ReadMyBlueprint does the wall counting. You can use both on the same job.
