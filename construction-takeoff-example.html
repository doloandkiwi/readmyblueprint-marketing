Construction takeoff example: a worked drywall and metal stud count

By The ReadMyBlueprint Team · Nov 24, 2026 · Updated Nov 24, 2026

This construction takeoff example counts two interior walls, step by step. Both are framed with 3-5/8 inch metal studs at 16 inches on center and covered with 5/8 inch Type X board on both sides. The result is 53 studs, 127 linear feet of track, 44 sheets of 4x8 board and 629 square feet of insulation.

Made-up example. These walls, dimensions and the 10% waste are invented to show the method. They are not from a real project. Use your own plans, details and waste factor. The same formulas are in the free construction takeoff template.

What do you write down from the plans first?

One line per wall, with its sheet and wall type. Our example (sheet A-101, invented):

  • Wall A, type A: 40 ft long, 10 ft high, one 3 ft x 7 ft door.
  • Wall B, type B: 25 ft long, 10 ft high, no openings.

Both: 3-5/8 inch studs at 16 inches on center, 5/8 inch Type X on both sides. Check the scale on the sheet before measuring; every number below depends on it.

How many studs does each wall need?

Studs = length x 12 / spacing, rounded up, plus 1.

  • Wall A: 40 x 12 = 480 inches. 480 / 16 = 30 spaces. 30 + 1 = 31 studs. The door adds 2 king studs in this example: 33.
  • Wall B: 25 x 12 = 300 inches. 300 / 16 = 18.75, rounded up to 19. 19 + 1 = 20 studs.
  • Total: 33 + 20 = 53 studs.

This example ignores headers and cripple studs. Your door details decide how many extras a real opening needs.

How much track does each wall need?

Track runs along the top and the bottom, and the bottom track stops at the door.

  • Wall A: top 40 ft + bottom (40 - 3) 37 ft = 77 linear feet.
  • Wall B: 25 + 25 = 50 linear feet.
  • Total: 127 linear feet, or 13 pieces of 10 ft track (12.7 rounded up).

How many sheets of drywall do the walls need?

Board area = (length x height - openings on that side) x sides covered.

  • Wall A: 40 x 10 = 400 sq ft per side. Minus the door (3 x 7 = 21) = 379. Two sides: 758 sq ft.
  • Wall B: 25 x 10 = 250 sq ft per side. Two sides: 500 sq ft.
  • Total board: 758 + 500 = 1,258 sq ft.

How do waste and rounding change the count?

Add waste, divide by the sheet size, then round up once. With the example 10% waste: 1,258 x 1.10 = 1,383.8 sq ft. A 4x8 sheet covers 32 sq ft, so 1,383.8 / 32 = 43.2, which rounds up to 44 sheets.

Rounding each wall separately gives 27 + 18 = 45 sheets, one more than rounding the project total. On a job with many short walls that gap grows, so decide which way you round before you compare numbers with a supplier. For choosing a waste figure, see drywall waste factor.

How much insulation do the walls need?

If both walls get cavity insulation, the cavity area is the wall area on one side, net of openings: Wall A 379 sq ft plus Wall B 250 sq ft is 629 sq ft, before waste. Convert to batts or bags using the coverage printed on the product you buy.

What does the finished takeoff look like?

  • 3-5/8 inch metal studs, 10 ft: 53 each
  • 3-5/8 inch track, 10 ft: 127 linear feet (13 pieces)
  • 5/8 inch Type X, 4x8: 44 sheets, including the example 10% waste
  • Cavity insulation: 629 sq ft, before waste

No prices, because it is a takeoff. Pricing it is the estimate.

How do you check the numbers?

Three quick checks catch most errors. Board square feet should be close to wall area times sides. The sum of wall lengths should match the walls you can trace on the sheet. And every row should have a sheet reference so you can find the wall again. On a real set the big risk is a wall that is missing, not a wrong formula.

Scanned plans are counted too. Every set is counted by three independent automated passes, each checked against the others, and every line names the sheet it came from.
