Drywall Sheet Weight Chart: Pounds per Sheet by Type and Thickness

By The ReadMyBlueprint Team · Nov 25, 2026 · Updated Nov 25, 2026

A 4x8 sheet of 1/2 inch regular drywall weighs about 51 pounds, and a 4x8 sheet of 5/8 inch Type X weighs about 70 to 74 pounds. Those figures come from the manufacturers' nominal weights of 1.6 lb per square foot for 1/2 inch regular board and 2.2 to 2.3 lb per square foot for 5/8 inch Type X. A 4x10 sheet is 25% heavier than a 4x8 of the same board and a 4x12 is 50% heavier, because the area is 40 and 48 square feet against 32.

The chart below lists nominal weight by board type and thickness from USG, CertainTeed, Georgia-Pacific and National Gypsum, then converts each to pounds per 4x8, 4x10 and 4x12 sheet. Where a manufacturer's data sheet we could open did not list a product, the cell says "not checked" rather than a guess.

Rule of thumb from the table: 1,000 square feet of 1/2 inch regular board is about 1,600 lb, and 1,000 square feet of 5/8 inch Type X is about 2,200 to 2,300 lb. That is roughly 31 sheets of 4x8 per 1,000 square feet.

What changes the weight of a sheet

  • Thickness: going from 1/2 inch regular (1.6) to 5/8 inch Type X (2.2 to 2.3) adds 0.6 to 0.7 lb per square foot, or 19 to 22 lb on a 4x8.
  • Lightweight cores: the lightweight 1/2 inch boards in the chart run 1.2 to 1.45 lb per square foot, so a 4x8 is roughly 38 to 46 lb instead of 51. USG's lightweight 5/8 inch Type X is listed at 1.8 against 2.2 for its standard Firecode X.
  • Abuse resistance: the abuse-resistant 5/8 inch Type X boards are listed at 2.8 to 2.9, about 27% to 32% heavier than standard 5/8 inch Type X at 2.2.
  • The plant it came from: Georgia-Pacific's submittals say the listed weight is approximate for design and shipping and may vary with manufacturing location. USG's catalog says the same and tells readers to ask the local representative for the weight in their area.

Using the weights on a job

Weight matters for delivery and staging, not for the count. A takeoff counts sheets and square feet by board type, and the weight chart is a second step: sheets per type times pounds per sheet gives the load to plan for a floor, a lift or a truck. A 4x12 sheet of 5/8 inch Type X is about 106 to 110 lb. Ask the supplier for the actual weight on the delivery ticket, because the numbers here are nominal.

Weights on a manufacturer's submittal are for the board only. Joint compound, screws and tape are extra, and they are not in this chart.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a sheet of 1/2 inch drywall weigh?

About 51 lb for a 4x8 sheet of regular 1/2 inch board (1.6 lb per square foot times 32 square feet), about 64 lb for a 4x10 and about 77 lb for a 4x12. Lightweight 1/2 inch board is 1.2 to 1.45 lb per square foot, so a 4x8 is about 38 to 46 lb.

How much does a sheet of 5/8 inch Type X drywall weigh?

About 70 to 74 lb for a 4x8, 88 to 92 lb for a 4x10 and 106 to 110 lb for a 4x12, based on 2.2 to 2.3 lb per square foot from USG, CertainTeed, Georgia-Pacific and National Gypsum.

How much does a 4x12 sheet of drywall weigh?

Multiply the board's weight per square foot by 48. A 4x12 of 1/2 inch regular is about 77 lb, of 5/8 inch Type X about 106 to 110 lb, and of 5/8 inch abuse-resistant Type X about 134 to 139 lb.

Is 5/8 inch drywall much heavier than 1/2 inch?

Yes. 5/8 inch Type X is 2.2 to 2.3 lb per square foot against 1.6 for 1/2 inch regular board, which is 19 to 22 lb more on a 4x8 sheet. Lightweight 5/8 inch Type X (USG lists 1.8) closes part of that gap.

How many 4x8 sheets of drywall weigh 1,000 pounds?

About 20 sheets of 1/2 inch regular board (51 lb each) or about 14 sheets of 5/8 inch Type X (70 to 74 lb each). Weights are nominal, so use the delivery ticket for a load calculation.

Why do manufacturers list different weights for the same board?

Weights are nominal and vary by plant and formulation. Georgia-Pacific says its figures are approximate for design and shipping and can vary by manufacturing location. CertainTeed's own documents list 2.2 lb per square foot for 5/8 inch Type X in a submittal and 2.3 in a ProRoc overview.
