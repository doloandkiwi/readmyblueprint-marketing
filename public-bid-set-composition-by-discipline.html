What's in a Public Bid Set? 318 Pages, 9 Missouri Sets

By The ReadMyBlueprint Team · Nov 10, 2026 · Original data

Across 9 public Missouri state bid sets (318 pages), only about 3 in 10 pages (98, or 31%) were architectural sheets, and about half of all sheets (166, or 52%) printed a scale that software can read. Civil, structural, mechanical, electrical, plumbing and general sheets filled the rest. The sample is small and comes from one state, so read it as a snapshot of 9 sets, not an industry average.

The counts

Everything below is a count of sheets, not feet or dollars. Each set is a public bid set with interior wall work, listed on the Missouri Office of Administration plan listing while bids were open. We scanned them in October 2026 and re-checked the page and scale counts on Nov 10, 2026.

What stood out when we looked closer

  • A set can have no scale at all in its text. Set A has 7 pages of real vector linework (its last sheet alone holds thousands of drawn paths) and not one sheet states a scale such as 1/8" = 1'-0" in its text layer. Software cannot turn those lines into feet without a known dimension to calibrate from. We have not looked at why: it may use graphic scale bars or "as noted" callouts.
  • Most pages of a big set are not where you measure walls. In the 133-page set, 29 pages are civil and 21 are structural, against 31 architectural. A takeoff that starts on page 1 and works through every sheet spends most of its time on pages with no partitions.
  • Keyword legend detection is loose. Our engine flags a sheet as a legend when it finds words like "partition types" or "wall schedule." In one set it flagged a sheet titled Mechanical Schedules, which has no wall types on it. That is why our other bid-set page reports "legend sheet detected" separately from "partition type we could read."
  • Sheet numbers are not guaranteed. In one 36-page set our reader found no sheet number on 6 sheets, so those 6 fall outside the discipline counts and show up as unlabeled.
For a wall takeoff: the pages that matter are usually a small slice of the set. Find the floor plans, the enlarged plans and the wall-type or partition sheet first, check that each has a scale, and only then measure. How to read a wall type legend and the architectural scale chart cover the two things you need before measuring a wall. We did not count how often CMU or rated walls are drawn on structural or general sheets instead (not checked).

How we counted

  • Source: free public plan PDFs from the Missouri Office of Administration bid listing (oa.mo.gov, checked Nov 10, 2026). We kept projects whose titles suggest interior wall work.
  • Pages: PDF page count, rechecked on Nov 10, 2026. All 9 matched our October scan.
  • Discipline: from the letter prefix of each sheet number (A for architectural, S for structural, and so on). Demolition sheets with an architectural prefix are grouped with architectural here.
  • Readable scale: a sheet counts if its text contains a ratio such as 1/8" = 1'-0". Our engine's own scale reader and a separate text search both gave 166 sheets in total, and in every set the two differed by one sheet or less. Both read the PDF text, so a scale that exists only as a picture or a graphic bar would be missed by both.
  • Wall-plan sheets: our engine's classification, not a hand count.

Limits

  • 9 sets, one state agency, open bids only. Private commercial and residential plans will look different.
  • We picked sets with interior wall work, so civil-only and mechanical-only bids are not in the sample.
  • The engine builds used in October and in the re-check may not be identical.
  • Not checked: whether the printed scales are correct, whether sheets with no scale needed one, and how many sheets a person would call wall plans.
  • These are counts of sheets. They say nothing about how well any software measures walls. The one published test of that is Freeport Square, a 21-page apartment set where 68.9% of walls were auto-matched and 99.8% after one "Confirm all." That is a single data point, not a promise about your plans (how we word accuracy).

Frequently asked questions

How many pages are in a typical public bid set?

In the 9 public Missouri state bid sets we checked, the median was 26 pages and the range was 7 to 133 pages (318 pages in total). The sample is one state agency's open bids that include interior wall work, so it is not a national figure.

What share of a bid set is architectural sheets?

98 of 318 pages (31%) were architectural or architectural demolition sheets. Per set the median was 36% and the range was 22% to 86%. Civil, structural, mechanical, electrical, plumbing and general sheets made up most of the rest.

Do all sheets in a bid set state a drawing scale?

No. 166 of 318 sheets (52%) printed a scale in a form such as 1/8 in. = 1 ft. 0 in. that software can read from the PDF text. By set it ranged from 0% (a 7-page set with no scale text on any sheet) to 72%.

How many sheets in a bid set show the walls you count?

Our engine classed 12 of 318 pages (4%) as wall-plan sheets, and 8 of 9 sets had at least one. That is an automated classification and we did not hand count it, so treat it as a rough floor, not a precise number.

Does this data include customer plans?

No. Only 9 public Missouri state bid sets, downloaded from the state's free plan listing, were used. No customer-uploaded plan is in this dataset.
