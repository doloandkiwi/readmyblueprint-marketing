Metal Stud Sizes & Gauge Chart (Steel Stud Dimensions)

Sep 25, 2026 · Guide

Light-gauge steel studs are named by their web depth and their thickness (gauge or mils). Both matter for a takeoff: depth sets the wall thickness you'll see on the drawings, and gauge changes the price per stud and whether the wall can carry a given height or load.

Common stud sizes (web depth)

Nominal sizeActual depthTypical use
1 5/8"1.625"Furring, chases, soffits
2 1/2"2.5"Short interior partitions, furring walls
3 5/8"3.625"The standard interior partition
4"4"Taller partitions, plumbing walls
6"6"Tall walls, shaft walls, exterior curtain-wall backup
8"8"Very tall or load-bearing walls

Finished wall thickness on the plans = stud depth + board layers. A 3 5/8" stud with 5/8" gypsum both sides draws as a 4 7/8" wall.

Gauge and thickness

GaugeMilsApprox. thicknessTypical use
25 ga18 mil0.018"Non-structural interior partitions ("drywall studs")
20 ga (drywall)30 mil0.030"Taller partitions, tile backer, heavier board
20 ga (structural)33 mil0.033"Light structural, exterior backup
18 ga43 mil0.043"Structural / load-bearing, tall walls
16 ga54 mil0.054"Structural, headers, heavy loads
14–12 ga68–97 mil0.068–0.097"Heavy structural framing

Lower gauge number = thicker steel. Many specs now call out mils instead of gauge; when a legend says "20 ga," check whether it means the 30-mil drywall stud or the 33-mil structural one: they're priced differently.

Flanges and track

Studs usually have 1 1/4" or 1 5/8" flanges. Track (runner) matches the stud's web depth and runs along the floor and ceiling; deflection (slip) track is common at the top of partitions that stop under a floor or roof deck. Count track as twice the wall length in 10 ft sticks, plus deflection track where the details call for it.

How this shows up in a takeoff

  • Group walls by stud depth and gauge. Same depth, different gauge = different line items.
  • Check the wall type legend for spacing: 16" o.c. is common, 24" o.c. for some partitions, 12" for tall or tile walls.
  • Stud count ≈ wall length ÷ spacing + 1, plus extras at corners and openings. Try the metal stud calculator.
  • Heights beyond limiting-height tables (per manufacturer) often force a heavier gauge or deeper stud; the drawings usually specify it, but it's worth a check.

Quick answers

What gauge are standard metal studs?

Interior non-structural partitions typically use 25 gauge (18 mil) or 20 gauge drywall studs (30 mil). Structural and load-bearing framing uses 20 ga structural (33 mil), 18 ga (43 mil) or heavier.

What does 3 5/8 metal stud mean?

It's the stud's web depth: 3.625 inches. With 5/8" drywall on both sides, the finished wall is about 4 7/8" thick.

Is a higher gauge number thicker?

No, the opposite: 16 gauge is thicker and stronger than 20 gauge, which is thicker than 25 gauge.

What's the difference between 20 gauge drywall and 20 gauge structural studs?

Drywall (EQ) 20 ga studs are about 30 mil; structural 20 ga are 33 mil. They're not interchangeable on structural walls and are priced differently.
