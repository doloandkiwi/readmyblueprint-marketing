How to take off wood framing from a PDF floor plan

By The ReadMyBlueprint Team · Nov 5, 2026 · Updated Nov 5, 2026

To take off wood framing from a PDF floor plan, calibrate the scale, measure each wall by type from the legend, then price five lumber groups: studs, plates, opening framing, sheathing and blocking. Studs come from length over spacing plus one. Plates come from length times three. Openings and sheathing come from the schedules and wall sections.

What should be open before I measure anything?

Three things: the floor plans, the wall-type legend (or the wall sections that act as one), and the door and window schedules. The floor plan gives lengths. The legend gives stud size and spacing. The schedules give opening sizes, which drive the header and jack stud count. Calibrate the PDF against one printed dimension before you trace a single wall, because exported PDFs are not always at the scale on the title block (see why PDF plan measurements are off).

Which lumber groups go on the list?

Keep the groups separate. Mixing them is how a takeoff ends up with studs counted twice and no plates at all.

GroupWhat drives the quantityWhere it lives on the plans
Wall studsWall length divided by spacing, plus one, per wall typeFloor plan lengths, legend spacing
PlatesWall length times three (one bottom plate, two top plates)Floor plan lengths
Opening framingKings, jacks, headers, sills and cripples per openingDoor and window schedules, framing details
SheathingWall area divided by 32 sq ft per 4x8 sheetWall sections, general notes
Blocking and extrasCounted by detail: fire blocking, backing for cabinets, hold-downsDetail sheets, structural notes

Two code facts shape the plate line. Walls that carry load normally get a double top plate, and the joints in the two plates have to be offset by at least 48 inches (IBC 2308 top plate rule, as hosted by UpCodes). That affects how you cut stock lengths, not the linear footage. Spacing is the other one: exterior walls are normally framed at 16 inches, and 24 inches is allowed for 2x4 studs only in one-story buildings where the sheathing can span between studs (American Wood Council, lumber studs). Read spacing off your legend rather than assuming either one.

What does a full example look like?

Assumed numbers, not from a real job: a one-story rectangle, 40 ft by 24 ft, so 128 ft of exterior wall. 8 ft walls, 2x6 studs at 16 inches on center, 8 openings (6 windows and 2 doors) with a combined 160 sq ft of rough opening, 4x8 sheathing.

LineMathResult
Layout studs128 ft x 12 = 1,536 in; 1,536 / 16 = 96; plus 197 studs
Corners4 corners, 2 extra studs each (assumed 3-stud corner)8 studs
Opening studs8 openings, 2 kings and 2 jacks each32 studs
Stud total97 + 8 + 32137 studs
Plates128 ft x 3384 LF, or 24 sixteen-foot pieces, before waste
Sheathing, gross128 ft x 8 ft = 1,024 sq ft; / 3232 sheets
Sheathing, net of openings(1,024 - 160) = 864 sq ft; / 3227 sheets

Two judgment calls hide in that table. First, the kings and jacks are added on top of the layout studs, so where a king lands on a layout stud position the total is a little high; some estimators subtract one layout stud per king, and the right answer is whatever your bid practice says, written on the line. Second, gross versus net sheathing is a bid-practice choice too, since cutting around openings wastes most of what you deduct. State which one you used. The headers themselves are a separate count; see how to count headers, king studs and jack studs.

Where do wood framing takeoffs from a PDF go wrong?

  • A wall that shows on the floor plan, the framing plan and an enlarged plan gets measured three times. Count walls from the floor plans only.
  • Interior partitions at 16 inches and exterior walls at 24 inches (or the reverse) get one blended spacing. Measure per wall type.
  • The legend text in the PDF is scrambled or missing, so wall tags cannot be matched to a stud size. We hit this on our own test set; the write-up is when the legend is missing or scrambled.
  • A scanned plan set. Scans are pictures, so every length is a manual trace. See vector vs. scanned PDFs.

Frequently asked questions

How do I take off wood framing from a PDF floor plan?

Calibrate the PDF scale, measure each wall by type, then count five groups: studs (length divided by spacing, plus one), plates (length times three), opening framing from the schedules, sheathing from wall area divided by 32 square feet per 4x8 sheet, and blocking from the details.

How many plates does a wood stud wall need?

A standard bearing wall has one bottom plate and a double top plate, so three times the wall length in linear feet before waste. Joints in the two top plates are offset at least 48 inches, which affects stock lengths, not the footage.

Should I deduct openings from the sheathing count?

It is a bid-practice choice. Net area is smaller, but cutting sheets around openings produces offcuts you often cannot reuse. Pick one method, apply it to every job, and write it on the estimate line.

Can I do a wood framing takeoff from a scanned PDF?

You can, but every length has to be traced by hand because a scan is only a picture. Vector PDFs exported from CAD keep real line geometry, which is what lets software measure walls directly.

Does ReadMyBlueprint count plates, headers and blocking?

Not as separate lines. It measures walls, matches them to your legend and returns stud counts, plus sheet counts where the plans call out sheathing. Plates, headers, blocking and unlabeled engineered lumber stay on your own checklist.
