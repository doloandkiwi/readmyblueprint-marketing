How ReadMyBlueprint's Three-Pass Count Works

By The ReadMyBlueprint Team · Nov 10, 2026 · Method

Every plan set uploaded to ReadMyBlueprint is counted three separate times before its counts appear in your account. The second and third passes never see the first pass's numbers or each other's, and the three totals must agree within 3%. If they do not, a fourth pass traces the walls that differ. This checks that a count is consistent; it is not an accuracy percentage, and we do not publish one.

The passes

  • Pass 1. The software reads the plan set first. Pass 1 then goes through the drawings wall by wall: which walls are new and which are existing, what type each wall is from the legend and the tags on the plan, and its length.
  • Pass 2. A separate pass starts again from the drawings. It is not given the first total or the first wall list.
  • Pass 3. A third separate pass does the same, blind to the other two.
  • Fourth pass, only if needed. If the totals disagree, a fresh pass measures just the disputed sheets and wall types, and is told which ones are in dispute but not what the numbers were.

You upload the plan set yourself in the app. Until the checked counts are published your account shows "Your counts are being prepared," so the figures you see are the ones that went through these passes.

What every pass has to do the same way

  • Gross length through door openings. Wall length is measured centreline to centreline, straight through door and opening gaps. The headline number is never reduced for doors. Net-of-door lengths and run-through track are given as secondary figures. A pass that nets the doors is invalid and is redone, not averaged in.
  • Vector linework first. Each wall is measured from its two face lines in the PDF's own geometry, not by eye on a picture of the sheet. Only sheets with no vector linework (scans) are measured on screen, and the count says so.
  • One scope basis. The share of the plan that is confirmed is verified length divided by verified plus assumed plus not confirmed length, all on the same gross basis.

The agreement rule

We compute the spread of the three totals as the highest minus the lowest, divided by the average. It has to be 3% or less on the total, and also on every wall type or sheet that makes up more than 10% of the scope.

  • Within 3%: the median of the three is the number delivered.
  • Over 3%: the fourth pass runs. We then open the two wall lists side by side until the difference is explained. We never average a disagreement away.
  • Still over 3% after the fourth pass: only the walls where the passes agree are delivered. The disputed walls are listed as assumed, with the range and the reason.
  • Scanned sheets: with no vector lines to tie-break on, the agreement band is widened to 5%.

Where passes usually disagree

These are the causes we check first when two passes differ:

  • Door gaps counted gross by one pass and net by another.
  • An untagged wall that one pass counted and another skipped.
  • A wall shared by two rooms counted twice.
  • The overall floor plan and its enlarged plan both counted for the same walls.
  • Rounding at junctions: T-junctions end on the through wall's centreline.

Much of the work in a takeoff is on sheets other than the floor plan. Ceiling plans show bulkheads and soffits, sections show low walls and headers, and elevations show parapets. Each pass walks those sheet types as well, because a count that stops at the floor plan can agree with itself three times and still be short.

What you see in the result: every line names the sheet it came from, and anything the drawings do not settle is listed as assumed, with the one question that would settle it. See how we count and confidence labels.

What three passes do not prove

  • Agreement is not correctness. All three passes read the same drawings. A wall type misread from a cluttered legend, or an error in the drawings themselves, can repeat in all three. You still check the counts before a bid goes out.
  • No error rate for this process. We have not published one and have not measured one across enough plan sets to publish it. Not checked.
  • Not the same as the Freeport figure. The one published accuracy test is Freeport Square, a real 21-page plan set: 68.9% of walls were auto-matched by the software and 99.8% after one "Confirm all." That measured the software's wall matching, not the three-pass check, and it is a single data point, not a promise about your plans. See how we word accuracy.
  • Plans we cannot measure. Scanned or photographed plans have no linework to measure from, and plans with graphic partition details need more confirming. See wall-type matching.

Frequently asked questions

What is a three-pass count?

Every plan set uploaded to ReadMyBlueprint is counted three separate times before its counts are shown in your account. The second and third passes are made without seeing the first pass's numbers or each other's, and the three totals must agree within 3%.

What happens if the three passes disagree?

A fourth pass runs on the disputed sheets and wall types only, using vector linework measurement, and we trace the specific walls that differ instead of averaging the totals. If a dispute remains, only the walls the passes agree on are delivered, and the disputed walls are listed as assumed with the range.

Is a three-pass count an accuracy guarantee?

No. Agreement between passes shows the count is consistent, not that it is right. All three passes read the same drawings, so an error in the drawings or a misread wall type can repeat in every pass. You still check the counts against the plans before you bid.

Why are lengths measured through door openings?

Every pass uses gross wall length, measured through door and opening gaps, so all three are on the same basis. Net-of-door figures are given as secondary numbers. A pass that nets the doors is redone rather than averaged in.

Is ReadMyBlueprint a service where someone does my takeoff?

No. It is self-serve software: you upload your plan set in the app. The three-pass check is how we confirm the counts before they appear in your account, not a replacement for your own review.
