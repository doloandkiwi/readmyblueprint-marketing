How to estimate framing labor and cost: a method you can reuse

By The ReadMyBlueprint Team · Nov 24, 2026 · Updated Nov 24, 2026

To estimate framing labor and cost, start with a takeoff of the framing quantities, convert them to labor hours with your own production rate, multiply by your loaded hourly rate, then add materials with waste, equipment, overhead and profit. This page gives the method only. It has no wage or price figures on purpose, because those depend on your market, your crew and your supplier quotes.

Wherever the steps below say "your rate" or "your price", use your local numbers and your own closed jobs. Do not borrow someone else's.

Method only. Labor is quantity divided by a rate, so a wall missing from the takeoff is missing from the labor too. On Freeport Square (21 pages), 68.9% of walls were auto-matched and 99.8% after one "Confirm all." That is one plan set, not a promise about yours. Checking that every wall made it into the count is the step worth spending time on.

What do you need before you can estimate framing labor?

A takeoff, split by wall type. At minimum: linear feet of wall by type, wall height, stud spacing, stud size and material (wood or metal), number of openings, and the sheet each came from. Add site conditions that change production: floor level, access, material staging and whether the layout is long runs or many short walls. For a worked count, see the construction takeoff example.

How do you turn a framing takeoff into labor hours?

Labor hours = quantity / production rate. The production rate is units per labor hour, for example linear feet of wall framed per hour, and it should come from your own tracked jobs for that wall type. Keep a rate per wall type, because a tall shaft wall and a plain partition do not frame at the same speed.

  • Hours for a wall type = linear feet of that type / [your rate].
  • Crew days = total hours / (crew size x hours worked per day).

If you have no history yet, track the first few jobs by wall type and build your own rate table. A rate you measured beats a rate you read.

How do you turn labor hours into labor cost?

Labor cost = hours x loaded hourly rate. The loaded rate is the base wage plus everything you pay on top of it: payroll taxes, insurance, benefits and any other burden. Use your actual numbers.

If you pay or are paid by the piece, price the same quantity directly: quantity x agreed rate per linear foot, per square foot or per stud. That is a different pricing method, not a different takeoff. The trade-offs are in per piece vs per hour vs per square foot.

What changes the labor number?

Apply adjustments from your own history, not a guess: wall height, upper floors or hoisting, tight access, layout complexity (curves, many short walls, shaft walls), out-of-sequence work, and rework from drawing changes. Write the factor and the reason next to each one, so you can check it against the real job later.

How do you estimate framing material cost?

Material cost = quantity x (1 + waste) x supplier unit price. Take quantities from the takeoff: studs and track for metal, studs, plates and headers for wood, plus sheathing, blocking and fasteners where framing includes them. Get unit prices from a current supplier quote, since lumber and steel move. Do the waste and rounding step once for the whole project, as the construction takeoff template does.

What else goes into the framing cost?

The bid is a sum of parts:

  • Materials with waste
  • Labor (hours x loaded rate)
  • Equipment, hoisting and haul
  • Supervision and job overhead
  • Company overhead and profit

Write each as its own line so a change in one, such as a new supplier quote, does not force you to redo the rest.

Can you estimate a framing job per square foot or per linear foot?

Yes, as a check, not as the bid. Divide the final cost of your closed jobs by the quantity to get your own cost per linear foot or per square foot, then compare a new estimate against it. If the built-up number and the per-foot number disagree by a lot, look for a missing wall or a wrong production rate before you send anything.
