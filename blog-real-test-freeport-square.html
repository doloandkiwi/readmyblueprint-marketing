We ran a real 21-page apartment plan set through our own tool — here's exactly what happened

Sep 22, 2026 · 6 min read

Most "case studies" you'll read from a takeoff-software vendor are curated wins. This one isn't curated — it's a real end-to-end run against a real 21-page apartment building plan set (a multi-building project we're calling "Freeport Square" here), uploaded through the live product, results exported, numbers reported exactly as they came out. Including the part that didn't fully resolve.

The headline number

41.3%
of total wall footage auto-matched to a confirmed material spec
7,294 / 17,648
linear feet resolved out of the total measured

Every wall on the plan set was measured — that part worked cleanly, no errors, no crashes, real processing time, real CSV export. Of the 25 distinct wall types the plan contained, 2 (both wood-stud partition types) came back with a full matched material spec: stud gauge, track, drywall sheet count, insulation bag count, sheet citations and all.

MaterialLength (LF)Result
2x6 wood stud5,252.0Matched — 3,955 studs / 1,055 track, 1,975 drywall sheets, 362 insulation bags

23 other wall types (partition & CMU, various thicknesses)10,353.4Flagged "SPEC UNKNOWN — confirm manually," not guessed

The other 23 wall types didn't get guessed. They got flagged.

This is the part that actually matters more than the headline percentage. The remaining 23 wall types — the majority of the total linear footage — came back with an honest note attached to every single one: "no legend text matched this thickness — confirm manually." No stud gauge was invented. No CMU block size was assumed. The tool measured the geometry correctly and then declined to guess the spec it couldn't confirm.

We didn't stop at the number — we found the actual cause

Rather than publish "41.3%" and move on, we opened the plan PDF's own text layer directly to find out why. Here's what we found: this plan set's wall-type legend is printed on the same sheet as the door schedule, with the two tables' columns interleaved in the drawing layout. When text is extracted from that sheet, the door-schedule and wall-type-legend text comes out scrambled together instead of in clean reading order — a structural quirk of how this specific architect laid out that one sheet, not a generic flaw in how plan sets are typically formatted.

What the auto-reader saw Wall-type legend + door schedule text interleaved on one sheet
What that caused 23 of 25 wall specs correctly flagged as unconfirmable, not guessed
This is exactly the scenario our "Import a wall-type schedule from Excel" feature exists for. When the automatic legend read can't confidently resolve a sheet like this one, you can type the wall-type-to-assembly mapping into a spreadsheet yourself — TYPE / STUD SIZE / GYP THICK or TYPE / WALL WIDTH columns — and upload it instead of confirming each wall one at a time in the UI. Every row stays reviewable before anything saves.

What this actually means if you're evaluating a takeoff tool

  • A tool that never shows you anything to double-check isn't more accurate — it just gives you no way to notice when it's wrong.
  • "41.3%" is not a general accuracy number for this product — it's specific to one real plan set with an unusually laid-out wall-schedule sheet. A cleaner sheet resolves a much higher share automatically.
  • The real question to ask any takeoff tool isn't "what's your accuracy percentage" — it's "what happens to the readings you can't confirm." Ours get labeled and handed back to you. They don't get silently guessed into a number that looks clean on a spreadsheet.

We're publishing this the same way we published the real bug we found in our own insulation detection — because a specific, inspectable failure mode you can verify is worth more than a marketing number you can't.
