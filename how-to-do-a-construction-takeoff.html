How to do a construction takeoff: step by step

Sep 23, 2026 · Guide

A construction takeoff is the list of materials — and their quantities — needed to build what's on the plans. It's the foundation of every bid: if the quantities are wrong, the price is wrong.

1. Get the right plan set

Make sure you have the current revision, all addenda, and a digital (vector) PDF where possible — a scan can't be measured accurately, only estimated.

2. Find what drives your trade

For interior framing and drywall, that's the floor plans, the wall-type legend, the reflected ceiling plans, and wall sections. For masonry, the elevations and structural notes. Note which sheets hold which information before you measure anything.

3. Check the scale on every sheet

Sheets in one set often use different scales, and enlarged plans are common. Verify the scale against a known dimension before you measure — a wrong scale silently throws off everything on that sheet.

4. Measure: lengths, areas, counts

  • Linear: walls, track, trim, base.
  • Area: board, sheathing, insulation, ceilings, CMU.
  • Count: doors, frames, fixtures, openings.

Group everything by type (wall type, board type) so each line converts cleanly into material.

5. Convert quantities into material

Turn measurements into orderable units: sheets, studs, sticks of track, blocks, rolls or bags. Guides for the common trades: drywall sheets, metal studs and track, CMU blocks.

6. Add waste and check your work

Apply waste factors per material, then sanity-check: does total board area look right for the building's square footage? Are there walls you never assigned a type? A second look at the few walls you were unsure about catches most errors.

Doing it by hand vs. with software

By hand, a mid-size plan set can take a day or more.
