How ReadMyBlueprint works, step by step

By The ReadMyBlueprint Team · Oct 29, 2026 · Updated Oct 29, 2026

You upload a digital PDF plan set. ReadMyBlueprint reads the wall-type legend, finds and measures the walls on each sheet, matches each wall to a legend type, and returns studs, track, drywall sheets and insulation per wall type. Walls it cannot match are flagged for you to confirm, then you export the counts. Your first plan set is free, no card.

The results table from our Freeport Square test set: one row per wall type, one column per material, a label on every row.

Step 1: Upload the PDF plan set

Upload the whole PDF as it is. There is no tracing and no clicking walls. The file must be a digital (vector) PDF exported from design software such as AutoCAD or Revit. A scanned or photographed page has no drawing data to measure, so the tool says it cannot measure that page. If only some pages are scanned, the digital pages still work. The 21-page Freeport Square set took about a minute; a 100+ page set can take several minutes.

Step 2: It reads the wall-type legend

On every page it scans wall geometry, the printed scale and the wall-type text. The wall-type legend drawn on your own plan set is what each wall is checked against. If the plan set has no legend, every wall is still measured, but results mostly come back as needs confirmation. For how a legend reads, see reading a wall type legend.

Step 3: Walls are found and measured per sheet

Each wall is measured from the vector data: length and drawn thickness, calibrated to the sheet's own printed scale. Ceiling plans, elevations and details redraw walls already shown on the floor plans, so those sheets are set aside to avoid counting a wall twice, and you are told which ones. Every material line lists the sheets it was counted on.

Step 4: Walls are matched to a wall type

The drawn thickness is compared with the legend. One legend match gives matched. More than one gives ambiguous, with every candidate shown. No match gives needs confirmation. Nothing is silently assigned a best guess. See wall-type matching and confidence labels.

Step 5: Counts per wall type

The results table has one row per wall type and a column per material: CMU, metal, lumber, drywall and insulation. You get studs, track, drywall sheets and insulation, tied to the wall each came from. In the tour run, the 2x6 walls came back as 3,008 LF, 2,264 studs, 605 track, 1,131 drywall sheets and 208 bags of insulation. That is one test set, not a promise for yours.

Step 6: Flagged walls you confirm

Flagged walls arrive with a suggested type. You check them and confirm, one at a time or in one click with "Confirm all", with no re-upload. Your confirmation is recorded as yours, not relabeled as matched. In the Freeport Square run, resolved walls went from 68.9% to 99.8% after one Confirm all. Rows the tool cannot identify show as SPEC UNKNOWN and stay out of the totals until you confirm them.

One thing we hit in that run: the legend shared a sheet with the door schedule and the PDF text interleaved their columns, so the tool flagged walls instead of picking a material. Read the full account in the Freeport Square test.

Step 7: Export

"Export these results (CSV)" turns the table into a spreadsheet for Excel and your bid. The export includes the label column, so a reviewer can see what was verified and what was not. An unconfirmed row stays flagged in the file.

Our delivery policy. We only deliver counts verified 100% against the drawings, and we list what is missing and why. If a wall type, sheet or item could not be resolved, it is named in the delivery with the reason, not folded into a confident-looking total.

What it does not do

  • It does not read scanned, photographed or flat-image PDFs.
  • It does not auto-detect doors, windows, pipe or fixtures. Add those by clicking the drawing or with Manual count.
  • It has no live regional cost data.
  • It is assisted takeoff: you verify every bid before it goes out.

Frequently asked questions

How does ReadMyBlueprint work?

You upload a digital PDF plan set. It reads the wall-type legend, measures the walls on each sheet, matches each wall to a legend type, and returns studs, track, drywall sheets and insulation per wall type. Walls it cannot match are flagged for you to confirm, then you export to CSV or Excel.

What file does ReadMyBlueprint need?

A digital (vector) PDF exported from CAD or Revit. It cannot measure scanned or photographed pages and says so instead of guessing.

What happens to walls it is not sure about?

Each result is labeled matched, ambiguous (every candidate shown) or needs confirmation. Rows it cannot identify stay out of the totals until you confirm them.
