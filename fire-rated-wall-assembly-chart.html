Fire-Rated Wall Assembly Chart: UL U419, U465 and V438

By The ReadMyBlueprint Team · Nov 25, 2026 · Updated Nov 25, 2026

UL U419 and U465 are 1-hour, non-load-bearing steel stud partitions with one layer of 5/8 inch Type X gypsum board on each side, and V438 is a 1-hour steel stud wall that National Gypsum publishes on the same page as U465. They differ in the stud size and spacing the listing allows and the insulation it calls for, and U419 also has 2, 3 and 4-hour versions with more layers. Manufacturers publish example configurations of each, shown below, but the UL listing is the only document that sets what you can legally build.

This is a quick reference built from USG, CertainTeed and National Gypsum published assemblies. It is not a substitute for the listing. Verify the listing for your project in UL Product iQ (category BXUV, fire resistance ratings) before you specify, price or build any rated wall.

Each row is one configuration as the named manufacturer publishes it, not the full UL listing. A UL design usually permits a range of stud sizes, spacings, board brands and insulation, and a manufacturer's example picks one. The sources differ on some details for the same design number (for example, U465 shown with 3-5/8 inch studs by CertainTeed and 6 inch studs by National Gypsum), which is expected when each manufacturer shows a different tested option and is why the listing text controls.

Verify the listing for your project. Open the design in UL Product iQ or the manufacturer's assembly page and read the full text for stud minimums, board brand and type, insulation, joint treatment, fasteners and head-of-wall requirements. National Gypsum's own page says a design professional must evaluate and verify suitability, that stud sizes in the assemblies are minimums and that spacings are maximums. The authority having jurisdiction has the final say.

U419 vs U465 vs V438

  • U419 is the broadest of the three. USG's 2013 assemblies catalog lists U419 constructions at 1, 2, 3 and 4 hours, from a single layer of 5/8 inch Firecode X on 3-1/2 inch 25 gauge studs at 24 inch on center up to four layers of 1/2 inch Firecode C each side on 1-5/8 inch studs. The rating comes from the number of layers and the stud and insulation the listing pairs with it.
  • U465 is the 1-hour single-layer design that CertainTeed pairs with U419 for 3-5/8 inch and 6 inch studs at 24 inch on center, where both listings cover the same wall.
  • V438 is shown by National Gypsum on one page together with U465 as a 1-hour wall: 6 inch 20 gauge studs at 16 inch on center, 6 inch glass-fiber insulation, one layer of 5/8 inch Fire-Shield each side. We could not retrieve V438's own UL text because UL Product iQ blocks automated access, so treat its details as National Gypsum's published example.

The stud thickness detail that trips up bids

Many non-load-bearing steel stud designs specify generic studs by a minimum bare-metal thickness. UL says generic studs without an asterisk do not carry the UL Mark but must meet that minimum, for example 25 gauge for U465. Proprietary EQ (equivalency) studs can be thinner because they were fire-tested, and UL says they are certified under the Framing Members category (CIKV) and must carry the UL Mark. USG's U419 example uses a 3-5/8 inch EQ25 stud at 0.015 inch, which is thinner than the 0.0179 inch minimum for a conventional 25 gauge stud on our metal stud gauge chart. Swapping a generic stud for an EQ stud, or the reverse, is a listing question, not a price question.

What to check before you price a rated wall

  • The design number in the legend. Wall type legends often cite the UL design. The number tells you which listing text applies; the legend line does not tell you the board brand.
  • Board type, not just thickness. 5/8 inch Type X is not the same product as 5/8 inch regular. See the drywall sheet weight chart for the weight difference and types of drywall for where each goes.
  • Stud size, spacing and thickness. The listing sets minimum depth, maximum spacing and minimum thickness. See the metal stud and track dimensions chart.
  • Insulation and layers. Some ratings require specific insulation or extra layers. National Gypsum's notes say additional layers may be added to any assembly.

Frequently asked questions

What is UL U419?

UL U419 is a fire-rated non-load-bearing steel stud partition. The 1-hour version uses one layer of 5/8 inch Type X gypsum board on each side of steel studs at 24 inch on center, and the design also has 2, 3 and 4-hour versions with more layers.

What is UL U465?

UL U465 is a 1-hour non-load-bearing steel stud wall with one layer of 5/8 inch Type X board on each side. CertainTeed shows it with 3-5/8 inch and 6 inch studs at 24 inch on center and National Gypsum shows it with 6 inch 20 gauge studs at 16 inch on center.

What is UL V438?

UL V438 is a 1-hour steel stud wall that National Gypsum publishes on the same page as U465: 6 inch 20 gauge studs at 16 inch on center, 6 inch glass-fiber insulation and one layer of 5/8 inch Fire-Shield board on each side. Read the UL listing for the full requirements.

What is the difference between U419 and U465?

Both are 1-hour non-load-bearing steel stud walls with one layer of 5/8 inch Type X each side. U419 also covers 2, 3 and 4-hour ratings with more layers, and the two listings differ in the stud sizes, spacing and insulation they allow. CertainTeed lists U465 or U419 for some of the same walls.

Where do I find the official UL listing?

In UL Product iQ, under the BXUV category for fire resistance ratings, by searching the design number. Manufacturers like USG, National Gypsum and CertainTeed also publish assembly pages that show the design.

Can I use a different brand of Type X board in a UL assembly?

Only if the listing allows it. Many listings name specific board types or products, and a manufacturer's assembly page shows its own board. Check the listing text for the board and stud allowances, or ask the authority having jurisdiction.
