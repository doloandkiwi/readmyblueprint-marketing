Drywall waste factor: how much extra board to order

By The ReadMyBlueprint Team · Nov 3, 2026

Short answer: add about 10% to your board area for a simple job with long straight walls, and 12 to 15% for apartments, small rooms, many corners and soffits. Then divide by the sheet area and round up once, per board type.

Those percentages are estimating starting points, not a published standard. The most useful number is your own: sheets delivered, minus sheets actually hung, on your last few jobs.

The formula, in the right order

Board area x (1 + waste) / sheet area, then round up. The common mistake is rounding the raw sheet count up first and adding waste on top, which counts the same offcuts twice.

Worked example: 40 LF of wall, 9 ft high, one layer on one side.
40 x 9 = 360 sq ft.
At 10% waste: 360 x 1.10 = 396 sq ft. On 4x8 sheets (32 sq ft): 12.4, so 13 sheets. On 4x12 sheets (48 sq ft): 8.25, so 9 sheets.
At 15% waste on 4x8: 414 / 32 = 12.9, so still 13 sheets. On a small area, rounding moves the order more than the percentage does.

What pushes waste up or down

  • Room size. Small rooms, closets and corridors with many door openings produce more offcuts that cannot be reused. Long open walls produce fewer.
  • Sheet length. Longer board means fewer end joints on long runs. In a small room a 12 ft sheet gets cut down anyway.
  • Wall height. A 9 ft wall hung with 4 ft wide board leaves a 1 ft strip course. Whether that strip is waste or a usable piece depends on whether the next wall needs the same strip.
  • Ceilings. Light, diffuser and access-panel cut-outs, plus soffits and bulkheads, justify a higher number than walls.
  • Layers and board type. Second layers are staggered, so joints land differently from the first. Keep 5/8 inch Type X, moisture-resistant and standard board as separate lines, each rounded up once.

Apply waste to the whole job, not each room

If you add waste and round up room by room, a 10-room job can come out several sheets heavy, because each room rounds up on its own. Total the board area per type across the job first, then apply waste once. The exception is a job delivered in phases: round per delivery.

Do not use waste to cover a bad takeoff

A bigger percentage hides misses. The usual ones are a wall type read from the wrong legend, board-to-deck walls counted at ceiling height, and a second layer or the second side missed on a rated wall. Fix the area first, then apply waste. Our guide on how to estimate drywall sheets from plans walks through counting area; the drywall takeoff checklist lists the usual misses.

Drywall waste factor FAQ

What waste factor should I use for drywall?

Start at 10% for a plain rectangular job with long runs and 4x12 board, and 12 to 15% for apartments, small rooms, many corners and soffits. These are estimating starting points, not a standard; replace them with your own overage from past jobs as soon as you have it.

Do I apply waste before or after rounding up sheets?

Before. Multiply board area by (1 + waste), divide by the sheet area, then round up once. Rounding up first and then adding waste double-counts.

Should waste be applied per room or to the whole job?

Apply it to the whole job for each board type. Rounding per room overstates the order because offcuts from one room get used in the next.

Is waste the same for ceilings and walls?

No. Ceilings usually need more because cut-outs for lights and diffusers are common, soffits add small pieces, and offcuts are harder to reuse. Keep ceiling and wall board as separate lines with separate percentages.

Does 4x12 board really lower waste?

Often, on long walls, because fewer end joints mean fewer pieces cut to length. It does not help in a small room where the 12 ft sheet gets cut down anyway, and it is harder to carry through doors and stairwells.

Should 5/8 inch Type X and moisture-resistant board get their own waste factor?

Track them as separate lines because they are ordered separately, so each line gets its own rounding. Use the same percentage unless your history says otherwise.

Does waste include screws, tape and joint compound?

No. Waste on the board is not the same as consumables. Those are usually figured from board area (square feet hung), not from the sheet count.

Can takeoff software calculate the waste factor?

It can apply the percentage you set to the board area it measures. ReadMyBlueprint reads the wall-type legend and totals board by type, so you apply your waste number to each type's area, then confirm the result against the plans.
