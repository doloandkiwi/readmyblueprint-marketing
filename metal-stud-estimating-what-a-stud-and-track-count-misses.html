Metal stud estimating: what a stud and track count misses

By The ReadMyBlueprint Team · Nov 10, 2026 · 6 min read

Short answer: any metal stud framing estimating software can give you a stud and track count. The lines that make bids come in short are the ones that live in the details, not the wall-type legend: deflection track, bridging, blocking, jamb framing at openings and screws. Before you trust a tool, test it on those six lines using one finished bid.

Why vendor pages do not answer this

We read the current PlanSwift framing estimating page, one of the top results for this search. It is a fair product page. It describes a Drywall and Framing Starter Pack with prebuilt parts for metal studs, track and channel, assembly-based formulas for waste and labor, and automatic takeoff features that it says need the estimator's review. What it does not do is tell a buyer where a metal stud count usually falls short. It does not mention wall-type legends, deflection track or bridging, it gives no worked example, and it does not say which plan formats a feature will not work on. That is normal for a product page, but it leaves the real buying question open.

This page covers that gap, and is just as direct about our own limits.

The six lines to test

  • 1. Gauge and depth as separate rows. A 3-5/8 in. 20-ga stud and a 6 in. 18-ga stud are different purchases. If a tool blends them into one "steel stud" total, you cannot order from it. See the metal stud gauge chart for how the sizes differ.
  • 2. Spacing per assembly. The same stud at 16 in. and 24 in. on center gives different counts. Spacing should come from the wall type, not a single project default.
  • 3. Track tied to the wall it came from. If track is a lump sum, you cannot check it. It should trace to the run it belongs to.
  • 4. Deflection (slip) track at the head. Partitions that stop under a deck often need it. Ask whether the tool reads it from the legend, from a head-of-wall detail, or not at all.
  • 5. Bridging, blocking and jamb framing. These show up in details and door and window schedules. Ask where the tool gets them.
  • 6. Screws and clips. Often left to an allowance. Ask whether the tool quantifies them or you do.
The test: pick a bid you already finished and run it through the tool. Compare line by line against your own sheet, and write down which of the six the tool covered, which it left to you, and which it got wrong. Do not score it on the total alone, because an error in one line can cancel out an error in another.

What we cover and what we do not

ReadMyBlueprint reads a digital PDF, measures each wall from the drawing's vector geometry, and matches its drawn thickness to the wall-type legend to find the gauge and spacing. Each gauge and spacing combination gets its own row, and track is derived from the same wall run. Against the six lines above, here is how that looks.

  • Lines 1 to 3: covered. Separate rows by gauge and spacing, track from the same run, each row labeled matched, ambiguous or needs confirmation.
  • Line 4: partly. Deflection track is counted only when the wall-type legend specifies it. If it is only in a head-of-wall detail, we do not pick it up.
  • Line 5: not covered. Bridging, blocking and other mid-wall reinforcement called out in a detail are not picked up automatically.
  • Line 6: not covered. Fasteners and clips are not quantified.

So on a metal stud job you would still add lines 4 to 6 yourself. We would rather say that here than have you find it after the bid. Our metal framing takeoff page lists the same limits.

A failure mode worth knowing about

On our Freeport Square test set (21 pages), 68.9% of walls matched automatically and 99.8% resolved after one "Confirm all". Two limits matter for a metal stud buyer. First, that set's own material was wood, so it tests wall matching, not a metal stud count. Second, the walls that stayed unmatched were mostly exterior walls and openings the legend never lists.

A partition drawn at a thickness with no legend entry comes back as its own flagged row, for example an unlabeled 3.6 in. partition marked "gauge and spacing not found on legend". It is never given a best guess, and you confirm it against the sections. A tool that quietly assigns a gauge to those walls gives you a cleaner-looking total that you cannot check. Which tool for which job

  • You want to build every assembly yourself, with full control of formulas: an assembly-based platform such as PlanSwift fits, and it can cover lines 4 to 6 if you build them into your assemblies. You do the measuring and the setup.
  • You want a first pass on wall counts to review: an automated tool such as ReadMyBlueprint saves the tracing, and you add the detail-driven lines. It needs digital PDFs and says so when a page is scanned.
  • You only need a stud count for one room: a calculator is enough. See the metal stud calculator.

Common questions

Does metal stud framing estimating software count deflection track and bridging?
Not automatically in most tools. These usually come from details, not the legend. Check where your tool gets them. Ours counts deflection track only when the legend specifies it and does not pick up bridging or blocking from details.

What should a metal stud takeoff separate into its own lines?
Each gauge and depth at its own spacing, track counted separately from studs, and any wall the legend does not cover as its own flagged row.

Does ReadMyBlueprint count screws and clips?
No. It counts studs and track only.
