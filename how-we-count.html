How we count: from your PDF to counts you can check

By The ReadMyBlueprint Team · Nov 4, 2026 · 5 min read

A takeoff number is only useful if you can see where it came from. This page explains, step by step, how a plan set becomes a list of counts at ReadMyBlueprint, what you can check on every line, and what we do when the drawings do not give a clear answer. It also lists what we do not claim.

The process in seven steps

  1. You upload a vector PDF. Plans exported from CAD keep their linework as real geometry, which is what makes exact measurement possible. Scanned or photographed plans are only pictures, so they cannot be measured automatically (see vector vs. scanned PDFs).
  2. The software measures the wall linework at the drawing's scale. It reads the scale printed on each sheet and measures from the drawing itself, not from a description of what a wall probably is.
  3. It reads the wall-type legend. Measured wall thickness is matched against the wall types printed on your own plans (see wall-type matching). Every result carries a confidence label: matched, ambiguous or needs confirmation.
  4. Our team runs repeated, independent counting passes. The passes are run separately and must agree before a count is released.
  5. Every line names its source sheet. Each line in the counts list shows the sheet it came from, so you can open that sheet and check it yourself.
  6. Anything the drawings do not settle is labeled. Items we could not confirm are listed as assumed or excluded, each with the reason, rather than being counted quietly.
  7. You confirm before you use it. The counts are an assisted takeoff, not a finished bid. You review the flagged items, confirm or correct them, and then use the numbers. If you find a mistake, send it to us and the counts are corrected. Corrections are included.
What a line looks like: an item, a quantity and unit, the sheet it came from, and a status of confirmed, assumed or excluded with a short note. If a number is an assumption, the list says so on that line.

What we publish, including the misses

We would rather show you where counting fails than ask you to take a claim on faith. Our results are public:

  • The Freeport Square test: a real 21-page apartment plan set. 68.9% of walls auto-matched, and 99.8% resolved after one “Confirm all”. It is one plan set, run once, and not a promise about yours.
  • Accuracy, and why we do not publish a single accuracy percentage.
  • A real bug we found and fixed, described openly.
  • The changelog, which lists what is still not solved as well as what shipped.
  • What we found running public bid sets through takeoff, with its limits stated on the page.

What we do not claim

  • We do not claim a blended accuracy percentage, because one number cannot tell you which walls on your set are right.
  • We do not claim the software will match every wall. Plans drawn with graphic partition details, or with no legend, need more confirming by you.
  • We do not claim the drawings are correct. We count what the drawings show, and flag what they do not settle.

Questions people ask

Do you publish an accuracy percentage?

No blended percentage. We publish individual tests, misses included, as listed above.

What happens to items the drawings do not settle?

They are listed as assumed or excluded, with the reason. You confirm them, or tell us the answer, before you use the numbers.

What if I find a mistake?

Email support@readmyblueprint.com. Corrections are included.
