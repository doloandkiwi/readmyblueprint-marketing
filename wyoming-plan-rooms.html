Where to find drywall & framing bids in Wyoming: plan rooms and bid sources

Oct 8, 2026 · 6 min read

Short answer: there is no single place to find drywall and framing bids in Wyoming. The State Construction Department posts its bids on Public Purchase, the University of Wyoming posts bid advertisements there too, WYDOT lists its bidding areas on its own site, and Cheyenne posts bids on its city site. Private work mostly comes by invitation from general contractors. This page lists the sources we could confirm, with how each one describes its own access.

Where a page does not say whether plans are free or need an account, we wrote "not stated." Pages that were blocked or did not show bid information are left out and named below. We have no ties to any source listed and are not endorsing any of them. Bid sources change, so confirm on the site before you rely on one.

How public and private bids work in Wyoming

Public projects are advertised by the owner: a state agency, a city or county, a university or a school district. The bid notice, plans, specifications and addenda are normally posted on the owner's own site, bidding platform or plan room, and every project shows its bid date. Read each notice for bonding and prequalification terms.

Private projects are usually bid by invitation. A general contractor or owner decides who receives the documents, and the plans typically sit in a regional plan room, a bid service, or the GC's own portal. As a drywall, framing, insulation or CMU sub you usually price your scope to the GCs who are bidding, so being on the right planholder list matters.

Tips for subs

  • Register as a planholder through the official channel. That is how you receive addenda, and some owners publish planholder lists that GCs use to find subs. Where a portal offers alerts by category or code, turn them on.
  • Go to the pre-bid meeting when there is one. Whether a meeting exists, and whether attendance is mandatory, is stated project by project in the notice. Read how scope is split between trades while you are there.
  • Track addenda until bid day. Wall types, ceiling heights and scope can change, so reprice from the final set and re-check the day before the bid is due.
  • Get the official set. Bid notices say which source holds the controlling documents. Plans from another source may be incomplete.
  • Check plan quality before you commit. Open the PDF and try to select a line or text. If you can, it is a vector file that can be measured exactly. If the page behaves like one picture, it is a scan and will take longer by hand. Also confirm the wall type legend is on the set; see what happens when it is missing.
  • Check bonding and licensing before you price. Public notices often call for bid security and proof of licensing. Read the notice for the exact form.

FAQ

Where do I find public construction bids in Wyoming?

There is no single place. The State Construction Department and the University of Wyoming post bids on Public Purchase, WYDOT lists its bidding areas on its own site, and Cheyenne posts bids on its city site. The table above lists each source and how it describes its access.

Do I need to register to see Wyoming State Construction Department bids?

The department's bid listings page says its bids are posted on Public Purchase and describes creating a free vendor account and choosing commodity codes to receive notices. The University of Wyoming's page says you must register with both Public Purchase and the university to download its documents.

Is there one statewide plan room or builders exchange for Wyoming?

Our check did not find one. We could not confirm an AGC of Wyoming plan room or a Wyoming builders exchange. The sources we read point to Public Purchase for state and university bids.

Can a subcontractor bid on Wyoming public projects?

Public owners advertise the prime contract, so subs normally price their scope to the general contractors who are bidding it. Read each bid notice for the owner's own rules, including any prequalification, certification or bonding terms.

What is a planholder and why register as one?

A planholder is a firm that has officially requested the bid documents for a project. Registering usually puts you on the list that receives addenda, and some owners publish planholder lists that general contractors use to find subs. Download plans through the official channel, not from a forwarded link.

How are private bids different from public bids?

Public projects are advertised openly and the documents are usually posted on the owner's site or platform. Private work is usually bid by invitation from a general contractor or owner, and documents come through a regional plan room, a bid service, or the GC directly. Access differs by source.

Does a scanned plan set matter when pricing a bid?

Yes. A vector PDF exported from CAD can be measured exactly, while a scanned plan is only a picture and has to be traced by hand or estimated. Check whether your plans are vector or scanned before you commit to a bid deadline.
